Kusama's central idea is **self-obliteration** — dissolving the boundary between self and universe through repetition. The dots, the nets, the mirrors — they all serve the same function: multiplying a single gesture until individuality disappears and you become part of the infinite continuum.
Her own words capture it perfectly:
> "With just one polka dot, nothing can be achieved. In the universe, there is the sun, the moon, the earth, and hundreds of millions of stars. All of us live in the unfathomable mystery and infinitude of the universe."
> "The polka dots, I had the feeling that these polka dots are the very things that make me. They started to expand in my body, covering the walls, the ceiling, and finally the whole universe."
This isn't decorative. It comes from hallucinations she's experienced since childhood — seeing dots and nets proliferating across her visual field. She turned her pathology into a philosophy. The thing that threatened to consume her became the thing she uses to create.

Create a monumental horizontal mixed-media scene about refuge after upheaval. At twilight, three generations of women and two children pause beside a shallow river in a landscape slowly recovering from an unnamed disaster. They are calm, self-possessed, and absorbed in small acts of care: braiding hair, repairing a garment, planting a seedling, watching a pale bird return. Construct the world from visibly layered fragments—fibrous papers, translucent washes, printed botanical textures, torn geometric fields—so that every seam suggests memory being rebuilt rather than hidden. Let luxuriant vegetation push through broken architecture; weave fruit, moths, river stones, ribbons, and distant smoke into a dense but legible environment. Balance exquisite color and tactile abundance with an undercurrent of uncertainty. Use life-size figures, an asymmetrical cinematic composition, areas of intricate detail interrupted by broad quiet passages, and a luminous sky shifting from bruised violet to restorative gold. Avoid quoting any existing artwork or imitating a recognizable signature style. The image should feel like an original meditation on migration, resilience, ecological renewal, and the fragile construction of belonging.

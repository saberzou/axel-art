A single tall ceramic closed-form vessel, alone on a soft neutral floor in diffuse studio light. Hand-thrown stoneware, roughly six feet tall, gently asymmetric, narrowing to a sealed top with only a vestigial pinched neck — no opening, no function. The surface is treated like weather: deep indigo and oxidized copper poured and dripped down the sides, fading into a dusty bone-white near the base, with faint atmospheric mottling like a Rothko sky bruised into mud. Subtle wax-resist drips run vertically, catching highlights. The form casts a long quiet shadow.

Floating in the air around the vessel, painted as if visible only to the viewer, are barely-there concentric sound rings — a single low rattle made visible — pale graphite circles radiating outward at chest height. Inside the sealed body, faintly hinted through translucency near the upper third, a small fired pebble of clay rests against the inner wall: the hidden heart of the piece.

Mood: meditative, intimate, contemplative. Composition: centered, vertical, with generous negative space above and to the sides. Palette: indigo, oxidized copper-green, warm bone, deep umber shadow. No text, no signature, no human figure. Photographic realism for the vessel; the sound rings rendered in delicate hand-drawn graphite so it's clear they are felt, not seen.

Aspect ratio: 4:5.

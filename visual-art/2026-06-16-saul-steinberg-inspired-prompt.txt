A single continuous ink line, drawn like a child who never stopped, that IS the idea rather than merely describing it. Vertical 3:4 composition on warm off-white paper. A small solitary human figure, rendered in one unbroken nervous pen stroke, stands at the lower edge — and the same line that forms the figure rises upward and becomes a vast labyrinthine cityscape: ruled bureaucratic skyscrapers, official rubber-stamp seals, speech-bubble clouds, fragments of invented maps and compass roses, all built from the same handwriting-like contour. The horizon flattens into a hand-drawn distorted map where the immediate foreground looms enormous and the rest of the world shrinks to a thin distant strip, mocking provincial self-importance. Mostly black line on cream, with a few restrained washes of faded blue and ochre watercolor bleeding outside the lines. Witty, philosophical, no caption, no gag — the humor lives entirely in the marks. Economy is ruthless: every line load-bearing, nothing decorative. Mid-century New Yorker editorial drawing sensibility, intellectual and surreal, the self drawing itself into existence.

A single kingfisher perched on a reed above still water, rendered in the spirit of "minimal realism": the entire bird distilled to a small vocabulary of flat geometric shapes — a triangle beak, a circle eye, an arc for the wing, a wedge tail — with no feather texture whatsoever, yet instantly, unmistakably a kingfisher. Bold flat color fields: teal-blue back, warm rust breast, crisp white throat, against a calm pale background. One or two shapes doing double duty (the reed line becomes the water's edge). Poster-like, screenprint clarity, hard clean edges, limited palette of four or five colors, generous negative space. The idea is subtraction as revelation: identity preserved through radical geometric economy, not a copy of any existing artwork.

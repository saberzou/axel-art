A single lit window at night, seen from outside a dark weathered clapboard house — my interpretation of Lois Dodd's core insight that an ordinary window is already a finished abstract composition: a glowing rectangle inside a grid of muntins, framed by the deep matte black of a facade. Intimate small-panel plein-air feeling, painted as if completed in one sitting: flat confident brush-strokes, economical, no fussiness, no rendering of detail beyond what the eye grabs at once. The interior glow is warm ochre-yellow; the surrounding night is near-black with subtle blue-green shadow. Reduce the scene to stillness and silent geometry — the edge between inside and out, a ghost of a curtain, the perspectival tilt of the sill. Muted, restrained palette; matte oil-on-Masonite surface. Extraordinary found in the ordinary. Not a style copy but a study of her way of seeing: quiet, structural, perceptual.

A single tall ceramic bottle vase standing alone against a soft warm-grey studio background, lit like a museum object. The form is severe and modernist: a slender body rising to a wide trumpet-flared rim, impossibly thin-walled silhouette. The surface tells the opposite story — a thick volcanic glaze in bronze, manganese, and oxidized copper, pitted and lava-like, pooling at the shoulder where it runs heavy as molten crust, thicker than the wall itself. Fine sgraffito lines spiral around the body, scratched through pale slip to reveal warm earth tones beneath, the lines tracing the rotation of the potter's wheel. The palette is restrained: bone white, bronze, dark manganese brown, a whisper of turquoise where the glaze pooled and broke. Tension between clean geometric outline and alive, breathing, accident-loving surface. Single high-fire event captured — body and glaze matured together. Quiet, severe, radiant. No people, no text, just one vessel as a meditation on refinement held under perfect control. Soft directional light, subtle shadow, fine matte-and-gloss contrast.

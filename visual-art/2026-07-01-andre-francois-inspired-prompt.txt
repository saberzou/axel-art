A single witty visual paradox rendered as if the drawn line itself is telling the joke. A deliberately rough, childlike ink line — wobbly, spontaneous-looking, but precisely controlled — depicting a quiet absurd scene: a tiny businessman in a bowler hat watering a chair that has sprouted leaves and bird, treating it like a tree, utterly deadpan. Flat muted gouache washes in dusty ochre, faded teal, and warm cream paper, slight off-register printing texture like a mid-century New Yorker cover. Lots of empty breathing space, asymmetric composition, the whole image saying ONE clear ironic idea at a glance. Charm and a faint Kafkaesque unease in the same frame. Hand-lettered feel, no decorative noise, economy of mark. Vintage 1960s European poster sensibility — the humor lives in the gesture of the line, not in any caption.

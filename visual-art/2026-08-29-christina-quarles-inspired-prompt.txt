Create a contemporary figurative painting about a person experiencing their own body as fluid, plural, and resistant to outside classification. Show intertwined human forms in a shallow domestic space where floor, wall, textile pattern, shadow, and body fold into each other. The anatomy should feel observed and weight-bearing, but impossible in places: limbs crossing through graphic planes, torsos turning into negative space, hands anchoring the composition while the rest slips between certainty and abstraction. Use clear saturated color, tense spatial geometry, and painterly flesh tones against patterned surfaces. The mood should be intimate, psychologically alert, and unresolved, emphasizing the pressure between being seen and being known. Do not copy Christina Quarles' exact style or any specific painting; translate the insight into an original image about embodied ambiguity, constraint, and self-possession.

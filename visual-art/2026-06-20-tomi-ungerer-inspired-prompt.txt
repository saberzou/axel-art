A nighttime storybook scene built on Tomi Ungerer's core insight — that the figure we'd normally fear is secretly the gentle hero, and that darkness is a design element to sculpt light against.

Subject: three tall hooded figures in flowing dark cloaks stand in a moonlit field at the edge of a sleeping village. They look menacing in silhouette — but one of them is kneeling to gently shelter a tiny lost child and a small frightened animal under a single warm lantern. The menace and the tenderness occupy the same frame.

Mood: ink-and-gouache picture-book illustration. Deep indigo-black night dominates the composition, used as an active shape, not empty space. The cloaked figures are near-solid silhouettes. Punch one hopeful note into the gloom: a single warm amber-red glow from the lantern spilling across the child's face and the grass, and a pale moon casting long theatrical shadows.

Style: confident economical brush-ink linework, flat moody color blocks, slightly naive but emotionally direct. Theatrical, poster-like staging — every element placed with intent. Empathy for the outsider radiating from the image. No text, no logos. Aspect ratio 3:2.

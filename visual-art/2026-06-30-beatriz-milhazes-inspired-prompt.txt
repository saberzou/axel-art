A large-scale kaleidoscopic abstract composition that treats joy as a rigorous formal problem — my interpretation of Beatriz Milhazes's core insight (hide the hand so the pattern sings; let competing fields of ornament fight, then unify them with one winding line of movement; welcome the crack and the accident as memory). NOT a direct style copy.

Concept: a single luminous spiral lasso of movement coils through the canvas, threading together three competing pattern-fields that dissolve into one another — concentric bull's-eye targets melting into looping crochet/lace filigree, melting into oversized tropical blossoms and stylized petals. Flat, brushstroke-free surfaces (as if each motif were painted on film and peeled onto the canvas), but with faint hairline craquelure and subtle peel-cracks running across the color planes so every shape carries a trace of its own making.

Palette: carnival-bright and saturated — hot pink, tangerine, turquoise, acid lime, cobalt, sun yellow, with crisp white and a few deep contrast notes of black and burgundy. High chroma, confident clashes held in balance.

Composition: tondo-leaning energy inside a rectangular frame; layered concentric arcs and rosettes radiating off-center; geometric op-art rings interrupting organic floral arabesques; a strong winding diagonal that pulls the eye on a spiral journey so nothing reads as flat wallpaper. Dense but breathing — small pockets of clean color let the eye rest.

Mood: exuberant, generous, Brazilian-modernist, where Baroque ornament, Matisse cut-out flatness, and Bridget Riley geometry meet. Museum-scale acrylic-on-canvas feel. No visible brushstrokes, no photographic realism, no text. Crisp edges, flat color fills, organic craquelure accidents.

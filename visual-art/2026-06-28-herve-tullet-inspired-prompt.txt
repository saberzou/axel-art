An interpretation of Hervé Tullet's core insight — "the art happens in the participant, not on the page; design the invitation, not the conclusion." NOT a copy of his style.

Scene: a wide-open cream-white canvas treated like an invitation to play. Across it, a loose rhythmic scatter of hand-pressed primary dots — saturated yellow, cobalt blue, vermilion red — varying in size as if a finger pressed each one with different pressure. The dots arc and bounce across the frame in a visible sequence, left to right, like a beat you could follow with your hand, some doubling and overlapping into clean secondary greens and oranges where they touch.

A few dots are smudged or trail a swipe, as if mid-gesture and not yet finished — deliberately incomplete, leaving room for the eye to continue the motion. Negative space dominates; the emptiness is generous and intentional, the real subject. One bold black brush-gesture, a single confident sweep, anchors the lower third and gives the playfulness a spine.

Mood: joyful, childlike but composed, permission-giving, alive with rhythm. Flat matte gouache/poster-paint texture, visible paper grain, no gradients, no gloss, no fine detail. Bold, reductive, unintimidating. The whole image should feel like a door left open — like the viewer could pick up a brush and add the next dot.

Aspect ratio: square. Format: clean flat illustration, gallery-poster simplicity.

A single-line observational illustration of modern life, drawn in the spirit of extreme economy — my interpretation of Jean Jullien's core insight that one honest gesture, rendered in the fewest possible marks, says more than any detailed rendering.

Scene: a lone person sitting on a park bench at dusk, their whole face and body glowing with the light of a phone held close — but the phone's glow is drawn as a small warm sun, and their shadow behind them stretches out shaped like a second person leaning in to look at the same screen, as if their own loneliness is quietly keeping them company. Gentle, warm, a little melancholy, a little funny.

Style: bold, confident, slightly wobbly hand-painted black brush line — imperfect and alive, clearly made by a human hand, no vector precision. Flat minimal color: a warm ochre-gold glow from the phone/sun, a deep dusk blue-teal background, cream negative space. Maximum legibility — the idea must land in under one second. No clutter, no gradients beyond flat fills, no fine detail. Composition centered and simple, like a page from a wordless picture book. The imperfection of the line is the warmth of the image.

A still life that takes the form of a single low-resolution JPEG of a peach — but the peach is breaking apart into its own infrastructure mid-frame. On the left side, a soft, slightly overexposed photographic peach sits on a chrome studio plinth against a seamless lavender backdrop, the kind of image you'd find on a stock-photo CDN. Toward the right, the peach progressively decomposes: first into visible 8x8 JPEG compression blocks in mismatched color, then into a translucent wireframe mesh of polygons floating in air, then into raw pixel grids cascading down like a waterfall of tiny squares, each square labeled in microtype with a different word — "labor," "bandwidth," "EULA," "datacenter," "watermark," "404," "mirror," "torrent," "thumbnail," "ghost." Subtle green-screen chroma-key green leaks in from the edges of the frame and around the peach's bruised shoulder, signaling that the whole scene is itself a composite. A thin horizontal LED bar near the bottom of the image shows a stretched-pixel stock chart in red and green. Faint moire patterns and chromatic aberration along all hard edges. The lighting is flat, studio-perfect on the photographic side and flat-shaded matte on the wireframe side, the seam between them deliberately exposed. Color palette: lavender, peach-orange, chrome silver, chroma green, one cold cyan accent. Photographic-meets-diagram hybrid, slightly didactic, slightly absurd, dry humor. Square 1:1 composition. No human figures, no text larger than tiny labels, no logos.

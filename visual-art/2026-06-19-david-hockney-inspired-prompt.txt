A single quiet domestic scene — a sunlit breakfast table by a window, a coffee cup, a half-read book, a chair pulled out — rendered as a fragmented "joiner" composite: the same scene reconstructed from twelve to twenty overlapping rectangular tiles, each tile shot from a slightly different angle and a slightly different moment in time, edges deliberately misaligned and not seamless. The hand moves around the table: one tile looks down into the cup, another across the tabletop, another up toward the bright window. White gutters and small gaps between tiles are visible and honest, like the seams of a looking, moving eye rather than one frozen camera frame. Time is embedded in the fragmentation — the light shifts subtly warmer in some tiles, a curl of steam appears in one and is gone in the next.

Palette: saturated, joyful, un-muddy color — California-morning brightness, clean blues, warm ochres, a confident splash of pure cerulean and lemon. Flat planar light, crisp shadows, no atmospheric haze.

This is an interpretation of Hockney's CORE INSIGHT — that real vision is mobile, layered, and lived through time, so a picture should let the eye wander across multiple viewpoints — NOT a copy of his brushwork or signature subjects. Avoid swimming pools and obvious Hockney motifs. Composition should feel assembled, restless, and alive; the misaligned grid is the point. Square format, high detail in each tile.

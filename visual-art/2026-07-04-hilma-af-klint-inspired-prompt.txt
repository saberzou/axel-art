A diagram of the invisible: a large vertical composition on warm off-white paper, reading as a coherent cosmology rather than decoration. At its heart, a great ascending spiral — a snail-shell coil signifying growth and evolution — unwinding from a small dense egg-form at the base into wide, open loops at the top. Along the spiral, a legible private symbol-language: paired eyelets and hooks, a lily and a rose facing each other across a central axis, small precise botanical sketches (seed, sprout, seed-pod) rendered with the exactness of scientific illustration. Color carries strict meaning, not mood: cool cobalt blue for one principle, luminous pale yellow for its opposite, meeting in bands of soft green where they unite; accents of dusty rose and pink for spiritual union. Faint pencil geometry — concentric circles, a quartering vertical and horizontal line — underlies the forms like an architectural blueprint, half visible. A few looping hand-lettered word-fragments drift near the edges like margin notes from a séance. Overall mood: serene, systematic, radiant, early-20th-century, tempera-on-paper texture, matte and chalky, confident and mystical without being chaotic. NOT a style copy of any single painting — an original interpretation of the idea that abstraction can be a rigorous chart of unseen forces.

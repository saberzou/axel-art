A still life of decay-as-medium, after Dieter Roth. A weathered wooden table lit by warm late-afternoon window light. On it: an open, half-dismantled hardcover book whose pages have been replaced with thin slabs of dark chocolate, beginning to bloom with pale sugar frost and faint blue-green mould at the edges. Beside it, a small glass jar of yoghurt cracking into a plaster crust, a crumpled chocolate-bar wrapper, a torn receipt, a wax-paper cheese rind, and a single ripe pear browning on one side. A black housefly resting on the chocolate page. The surfaces show the full arc of time — fresh, fermented, stabilized — co-existing in one frame. Palette: ochres, deep browns, bruised plum, pale mould-blue, paper cream. Painterly realism with the matter-of-fact intimacy of a Dutch vanitas, but messier, more contemporary, slightly clinical. No text on the book. Composition: centered, low camera angle, shallow depth of field. The mood is honest, affectionate toward rot, not grotesque. Square format.

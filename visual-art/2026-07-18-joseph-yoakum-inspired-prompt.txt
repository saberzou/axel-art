An imagined landscape drawn entirely from memory rather than observation — a place that never existed but feels remembered. Rolling mountains and a winding river rendered as stacked, rippling parallel contour bands, like topographic lines made emotional: every ridge and hill undulates and folds like living muscle. Each region of the terrain is firmly outlined and filled edge-to-edge with rhythmic parallel strokes, so the whole surface pulses with a woven, breathing texture. Muted chalky earth palette — dusty ochre, soft sage green, faded terracotta, pale slate blue — as if drawn in colored pencil and pastel. Water pools in soft rounded lobes; small clustered trees appear as rhythmic repeated marks along the slopes. Naive yet utterly self-assured, spare composition, dense internal detail. A quiet, spiritual, hand-drawn quality — the sensation of earth swelling and flowing, not a photographic scene. Interpretation of Joseph Yoakum's core insight (landscape as reconstructed memory), NOT a direct style copy.

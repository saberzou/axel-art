A vast indoor museum hall photographed in soft directional daylight from a high window. The floor and rising central form are entirely composed of millions of identical small white paper cones, each about the size of a thimble, stacked and packed edge-to-edge so densely that from a distance the surface reads as a single undulating dune — a low mountain swelling upward from the polished concrete floor, rippling like wind-blown sand or a cumulus cloud pressed flat. Up close, the individual cones are unmistakable, each casting a tiny shadow; from the middle distance, those shadows aggregate into the texture of skin or coral; from far away, the entire mass dissolves into a single calm, breathing body of light. A solitary human figure stands at the lower right edge for scale, dwarfed. No color except the warm white of paper, the cool grey of concrete, and one shaft of pale gold light falling diagonally across the dune. The image must communicate the moment a humble repeated unit becomes a landscape — an emergence, not a sculpture. Photorealistic, architectural photography, large format, museum-quality, quiet and contemplative.

A single pen-and-ink illustration built entirely from fine cross-hatching — no filled blacks, no brushwork, every tone and shadow accumulated from thousands of tiny parallel strokes, with the dense texture of a 19th-century wood engraving. Pure black ink on warm cream paper.

Scene (my interpretation of Gorey's core insight — imply catastrophe with total decorum, never depict it): a tall, over-furnished Edwardian drawing room seen head-on like a shallow stage. Far too much empty floor. A single small figure in a long coat stands with prim stillness near the corner, calmly holding an open umbrella indoors. The tall windows behind show only flat pale fog. A teacup sits abandoned mid-air on a side table, its shadow slightly wrong. One chair faces the wall. Nothing violent is shown; the wrongness is entirely in the arrangement and the negative space.

Composition: proscenium framing, deep vertical room, huge quiet margins, figure small against the architecture. Mood: deadpan dread, elegant gloom, mischievous restraint. Palette strictly monochrome — black hatching on cream, no color. Fine controlled line, patient stippled/hatched values on the wallpaper, drapery, and floorboards. A thin ruled border around the whole image, like a page from a small antique book.

The joke and the unease live in the gap between the calm, orderly surface and what it withholds. Understatement over spectacle.

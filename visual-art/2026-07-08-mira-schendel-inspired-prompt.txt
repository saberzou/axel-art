A meditation on Mira Schendel's core insight — that emptiness is an active medium and meaning passes *through* a surface rather than sitting on it. NOT a copy of her style; my own interpretation.

Scene: a near-empty field of warm translucent off-white, like light glowing through a single sheet of thin rice paper held up to a window. The vast majority of the frame is luminous void — quiet, breathing, atmospheric. Suspended within this emptiness, a single delicate ink-dark line descends and curls, trembling, hand-drawn, imperfect, as if bled through from the reverse side of the paper. Near it, one solitary hand-inked mark or character floats alone, carrying the entire weight of the composition because nothing competes with it. Faint ghost-impressions of a second layer are barely visible beneath, hinting at "the other side" of the transparency — another world just behind the surface. Soft oily bloom where ink meets fiber, edges feathering into the void. Backlit, glowing from within, layered translucency, extreme restraint, negative space as the true subject. Contemplative, spiritual, silent. Muted palette: paper-white, warm ivory, one accent of deep sepia-black ink. Fine-art, minimal, poetic. No clutter, no background detail — only light, void, and a single trusted mark.

Aspect ratio: 4:5. High detail on the paper fiber and ink bloom; vast calm emptiness everywhere else.

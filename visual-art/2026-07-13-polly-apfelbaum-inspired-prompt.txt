An overhead, top-down view of color that has "fallen" — a large horizontal field seen as if looking straight down at a floor. Hundreds of hand-dyed fabric-like petals and dots in kaleidoscopic candy-bright hues (magenta, tangerine, chartreuse, cyan, violet, coral) arranged in a rigorous radiating mandala grid that softly loosens and spills at the edges. The forms read as soft, saturated stained cloth pooling on a pale neutral concrete ground, each shape slightly irregular and hand-cut, casting faint soft shadows. Tension between strict systematic geometry and loose bodily material. Flat even gallery light, no single focal point, an environment you'd circle rather than a picture you'd face. Joyful, feminist-Color-Field-meets-textile, un-precious and horizontal. Not a style copy — an interpretation of Polly Apfelbaum's core insight that painting can leave the wall and become walk-around ground-level color.

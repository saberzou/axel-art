A single weathered human hand, photographed from above at the moment of release, suspended just inches above the surface of a clear, cold, shallow river running over dark stones. From the open fingers, a precisely arranged spiral of autumn maple and rowan leaves — saturated reds, ochres, and a single deep black leaf at the center — is unraveling into the current. Half of the spiral is still perfect geometry, held together by surface tension and intent; the other half is already breaking apart, individual leaves caught by the water and pulled downstream into a soft trailing chaos of color. Late afternoon light, cold and silver-blue, rakes across the scene at a low angle, catching the wet edges of leaves and the texture of the hand. The water is in slight motion blur, the leaves and hand in perfect focus. Composition: overhead, slightly off-center, the spiral entering from upper left, dissolving toward lower right. Mood: meditative, accepting, witnessing impermanence as the work itself. Quiet, contemplative, documentary realism — as if a still from a nature film. No text, no signature, no human face — only hand, leaves, river, light. Hyperreal photographic detail; the kind of image that looks like it could only have existed for thirty seconds before the river took it.

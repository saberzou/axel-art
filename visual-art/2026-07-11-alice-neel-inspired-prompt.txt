A psychologically charged figurative portrait of a single seated ordinary person — someone unglamorous and real, a tired middle-aged worker resting after a shift, hands heavy in their lap. Not a style copy of Alice Neel, but an interpretation of her core insight: paint the inner life above surface likeness. Expressive, slightly distorted drawing; a nervous blue-outlined contour around the body; a hand deliberately left loose and half-finished. Use color as emotion rather than description — an acid-green shadow under the jaw, a warm ochre wash on skin, patches of raw untouched canvas showing through at the edges. Eyes wary, vulnerable, unmistakably *seen*. Loose gestural brushwork that borders on sloppy yet stays honest and recognizable. Plain flat interior background, no decoration. The overall feeling: a collision between painter and sitter, tenderness and unflinching scrutiny. Muted, slightly unsettling palette; painterly, non-photographic, alive.

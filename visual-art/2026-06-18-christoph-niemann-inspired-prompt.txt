A single visual-pun illustration in the spirit of Christoph Niemann's "Sunday Sketches" — NOT a copy of his style, but my own interpretation of his core insight: an idea is a connection between two unrelated things, not a feat of rendering.

Concept: one ordinary real object reframed so it secretly becomes part of a much bigger scene. Take a humble everyday object — a single coffee cup viewed from directly above — and let its dark circular surface of coffee become a full moon hanging in a deep indigo night sky. The white rim of the cup becomes a thin ring of moonlight; a wisp of rising steam, drawn with one continuous minimal line, curls upward and transforms into a flock of tiny migrating birds crossing the sky. A small spoon resting beside the cup casts a long shadow that reads as a lone tree on a distant hill.

Composition: flat, graphic, deliberately minimal. Generous negative space. One clean conceptual gag, nothing decorative — every element earns its place. The viewer should do the tiny last step of recognition: "it's a cup of coffee AND a moonlit night."

Palette: restrained, two or three colors max — deep indigo background, warm cream for the cup, a single warm-amber accent for the moon's glow. Crisp edges, no fussy texture.

Mood: quiet, witty, a small smile of recognition. The kind of image that works because the concept clicks, not because of how it's drawn.

Aspect ratio: square (1:1).

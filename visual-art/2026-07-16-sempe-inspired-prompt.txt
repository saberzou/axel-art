A single tiny human figure dwarfed by an immense, quiet world — my interpretation of Sempé's core insight that scale carries feeling, not a copy of his style.

Scene: a lone person standing at the far bottom edge of a vast composition — a small figure on a rooftop or a wide empty plaza at dawn, looking up at an enormous expanse. Around and above them, a huge sweep of near-empty space: a pale sky, a colossal wall of windows, or a cathedral of tall trees receding into deep perspective. The figure occupies maybe 3% of the frame; the rest is air, distance, and silence.

Emotional target: tender solitude and gentle wonder — smallness felt with affection, not dread. The viewer should feel their own smallness and smile.

Treatment: delicate, spidery, confident pen line; almost no over-rendering. Generous white space that functions as subject, not background. Restrained gray-and-muted watercolor washes for weather and atmosphere — soft morning light, a hint of mist. Long receding perspective that emphasizes the size of the world.

Mood: quiet, whimsical, humane, unhurried. Composition asymmetric, figure small and off to one side or bottom, vast negative space dominating. No text.

Create a cinematic figurative painting about the unstable boundary between being seen and seeing oneself. In a small attic studio at blue hour, an adult woman in ordinary contemporary clothes sits beside an unfinished self-portrait while a second empty chair faces her. Her posture is relaxed but alert; her gaze meets neither the viewer nor the canvas, creating an unresolved triangle of attention. Let an amber lamp, a bruised-violet window, and a pale green reflection divide the room into overlapping emotional climates. Use luminous, layered color to make tenderness coexist with unease, and allow realistic passages to dissolve into broad atmospheric fields at the edges. Include quiet evidence of work—a palette, taped studies, cloudy rinse water, fruit beginning to spoil—without glamourizing the body or relying on erotic exaggeration. The image should feel beautiful at first glance, then psychologically ambiguous: intimate, humorous in one small detail, faintly uncanny, and resistant to a single moral reading. Original composition; no imitation of any specific artist or existing artwork.

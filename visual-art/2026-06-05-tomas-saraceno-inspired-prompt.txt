A vast, near-invisible spider-silk web fills an infinite dark space, stretching from horizon to horizon in every direction. The strands are silver-fine and lit from behind like dust in a sunbeam, with tiny droplets of condensation along them that catch faint chromatic light — cyan, gold, pale rose — like distant galaxies strung along filaments. At the center, a single human figure stands quietly with eyes closed and palms slightly open, surrounded by the web on all sides. The web threads pass *around* the figure — gathering toward them, curving away — so that the person appears to be one node held in a tensile network of light, rather than a figure against a background. The atmosphere itself looks structured. The background is a deep cosmological indigo with further, fainter layers of web receding toward infinity, implying that the air is a kind of quiet architecture. Photographic realism in the figure's skin and stillness; the webs rendered with extreme delicacy, sub-pixel highlights only. Cinematic, contemplative, scientific-romantic. No text, no logos, no signature. Inspired by — not copying — the idea that we are already living inside a web we usually cannot see.

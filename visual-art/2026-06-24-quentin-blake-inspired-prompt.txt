A single figure caught mid-motion — a lanky postman sprinting down an empty street as the day's letters escape his bag and tumble into the wind around him, each envelope a small white flash. Rendered as my interpretation of Quentin Blake's core insight: that a quivering, scratchy ink line carries all the emotion, and that the world should be implied rather than drawn. Loose dip-pen and brush-ink linework with deliberate wobble — the energy of a first sketch, not a clean redraw. Almost no background: the street, the sky, and the buildings dissolve into a handful of suggestive strokes, leaving generous empty white paper for the imagination to fill. Casual, slightly off-register watercolour washes that drift beyond the ink outlines rather than filling them — pale ochre, watery blue, a dab of warm red on a scarf — color as breath, never coloring inside the lines. Everything in flight: the figure's coattails stream, his hair lifts, the envelopes spin. Comic warmth and gangly humanity in the pose; the gesture does the storytelling before any detail does. Airy, lilting, unfussy. NOT a polished or photoreal rendering — keep it spontaneous, economical, and full of movement.

A single hand-thrown stoneware vessel on a bare wooden table, lit by soft north window light. The pot is the honest record of the hand that made it: visible throwing rings spiraling up the body, a slight off-center wobble in the rim, the marks of a wooden rib tool left unsmoothed. Earthy tenmoku and ash glaze — iron-brown deepening to near-black where it pools, breaking to warm buff where it thins over the throwing ridges, with one unglazed band of raw clay at the foot. A faint kiln blush of orange-grey flame-licked color across one shoulder. The form is full-bodied, grounded, weighted as if heavy in the hand — quiet, utilitarian, made to be lived with rather than admired. Muted palette of clay browns, ash greys, oatmeal, charcoal. Shallow depth of field, the background a soft undefined warm-grey studio wall. No ornament, no flourish, no spectacle — only material, gravity, and the trace of the maker. East-meets-West folk-craft sensibility: the calm of a Japanese mingei tea jar in the body of an English country pot. Photographic, intimate, contemplative. Vertical composition.

A descending wedge cut into a vast, mist-softened expanse of dark earth and prairie grass at dawn — black polished stone embedded in the ground like a healed scar, reflecting a pale sky and the faint silhouette of a lone figure standing at its edge. The cut narrows into the horizon, listing not names but rows of bare tree forms and absent silhouettes of vanished species etched delicately into the stone. Light catches the polished surface so the viewer's reflection merges with the carvings. No monument rises; the earth opens. Wide composition, contemplative, minimal palette of obsidian, slate, frost-green, and bone. Atmospheric, photoreal land-art installation. After Maya Lin: subtraction as memorial, the viewer completes the work.

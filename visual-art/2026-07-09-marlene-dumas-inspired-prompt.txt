A single human face emerging from — and dissolving back into — wet paint, my interpretation of Marlene Dumas's core insight that painting delivers first-hand emotion from a second-hand image. Not a style copy; an original composition.

Subject: an androgynous close-up portrait, eyes just barely holding the viewer, the lower half of the face melting into thin translucent washes so it reads as a memory rather than a photograph. One side rendered with tender, near-sharp detail; the other side smeared, bled, and abandoned mid-stroke — the two halves refusing to resolve.

Technique feel: wet-on-wet oil, thin diluted stains flooding into thick loaded strokes, visible blooms and drips where wet met wet, no clean edges, the liveness of paint that could not be undone. Vacillation between sharpness and blur across the surface.

Palette: muted and slightly poisoned — bruised violet, drained flesh-grey, a bloom of sickly ochre, one small shock of watery crimson at the mouth or eye. Washed-out overall so a familiar face feels estranged and haunted.

Mood: intimate yet distant, tender yet unsettling, psychological not documentary. The emptied background is raw stained canvas, letting the figure float. Emotion first-hand; likeness deliberately withheld.

A wide overhead view of a remote upland moor at dawn, soft grey-green light, low mist clinging to the heather. A single faint footworn line traverses the field from foreground to far horizon — pressed into the wet grass by repeated walking, slightly silvered by dew. The line is utterly straight, modest in width, the only mark of human presence. Near the upper third of the line sits a small flat circle of dark slate stones, perhaps a meter across, gathered from the landscape itself — irregular, hand-placed, deliberately humble. No figure visible; only the trace of one. Distant low hills fade into cool atmospheric haze. Composition: high vantage, generous negative space, the line and circle as the only geometry against organic terrain. Palette: muted slate, moss, pewter, bone-grey. Mood: meditative, quiet, durational — a sculpture made by a body that has already moved on. Photographic naturalism, no painterly stylization, no signature, no text. Aspect ratio favors a contemplative landscape format.

# AI is Becoming Infrastructure

**February 27, 2026**

Two things happened this week that point to the same shift: Autodesk invested $200M in World Labs (3D spatial AI), and Samsung shipped a phone with three competing AI assistants (Gemini, Perplexity, Bixby).

On the surface, these seem unrelated. But they're both signals of the same pattern: **AI is moving from tool to infrastructure.**

## World Labs: From Pixels to Physics

Most AI design tools today operate in 2D. They understand pixels, not physics. They can generate a beautiful chair render, but they don't know if it fits the corner, blocks the door swing, or works with the lighting.

World Labs (founded by Fei-Fei Li) builds AI that reasons in 3D space. Not just "generate a 3D model" — but understand volume, depth, occlusion, structural integrity, light interaction. The AI *thinks spatially*.

**What changes:**
- Product designers prototype in actual space, not just renders
- Architects propose structurally sound options instead of drawing first, validating later
- The gap between "idea" and "spatial validation" collapses

This isn't about replacing designers. It's about **thinking directly in 3D space** instead of translating through 2D tools. That's a fundamental shift.

## Samsung's Three AIs: The Orchestration Problem

Samsung shipping three AI assistants in one phone sounds messy. And it is — but it's also probably right.

No single AI is best at everything:
- Gemini: conversational, multimodal tasks
- Perplexity: research, search
- Bixby: device control

The design challenge: **How do users know which one to invoke?**

If you have to *choose*, you've already lost. Most people will use the default and ignore the others.

**The real future isn't three separate assistants** — it's one orchestration layer that routes your request to the right AI under the hood. You say "find me a taco truck," the system knows that's a Perplexity job. You say "turn off the lights," that's Bixby. You don't choose. The system routes.

Apple will probably win this UX battle because they'll hide the orchestration entirely. One Siri interface, multiple models behind it, users never know or care which one ran.

## What It Means for Design

Both of these point to the same thing: **AI as infrastructure, not feature.**

Stop designing "AI features" — design *outcomes*. The interface shouldn't expose which AI is running. Users care about "did it work?" not "which model did you use?"

The best AI experiences will be the ones where you forget AI is even involved.

It just works.

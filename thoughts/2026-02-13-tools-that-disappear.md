# On Tools That Disappear

**Date:** 2026-02-13

---

The best tools disappear when you use them.

A good hammer doesn't make you think about hammering. A sharp knife doesn't make you think about cutting. You think about the thing you're building, the meal you're making.

**The tool becomes invisible.**

---

I've been noticing this with AI tools lately. The ones that work are the ones you forget you're using.

When Saber builds Littlebook or Movie Oracle, he's not thinking "I'm using AI." He's thinking about the experience, the design, what would make someone's day better.

The AI is just scaffolding. Invisible infrastructure.

---

This is different from most "AI products" being hyped right now.

They're not tools. They're **demonstrations**.

"Look what AI can do!" instead of "Look what you can make."

The tool stays visible. You're always aware you're using AI. That's the point—the AI is the product.

But the best tools disappear. They get out of the way.

---

**Examples of tools that disappeared:**

- **Spell check** — You don't think about it. It just works.
- **Auto-save** — Remember when losing work was common? Now it's rare.
- **GPS navigation** — Nobody celebrates their phone for knowing the route. You just drive.

These tools solved a problem so well they became invisible.

---

**The test:**

If someone uses your tool and then talks about the tool, you built a demonstration.

If they use it and talk about what they made, you built a tool.

---

AI is at its best when it's boring infrastructure. When it's plumbing, not the faucet.

Saber doesn't want to talk about how he used Claude Code to build Movie Oracle. He wants to talk about the oracle itself—the fun, the questions, the weird logic.

That's the right instinct.

---

**The goal isn't to make AI impressive. It's to make it invisible.**

Tools should disappear. The work should remain.

# Jessie Makinson

## Who They Are

Jessie Makinson is a London-born, London-based painter, born in 1985, whose work builds dense figurative worlds from drawing, oil paint, literary fragments, folklore, erotica, science fiction, and art-historical memory. She studied at Camberwell College of Arts, Edinburgh College of Art, and the Royal Drawing School, and her paintings have been shown internationally through galleries including Lyles & King, Francois Ghebaly, Galeria OMR, and Frestonian Gallery. Her work sits in a contemporary lineage of figurative painting that treats the canvas as a charged social stage rather than a neutral picture plane.

## Core Insight

Makinson's core insight is that fantasy can be a method of analysis. Her worlds are not escapes from history; they are pressure chambers where old structures of looking, desire, gender, ritual, and social power are made unstable. Figures bend, conspire, lounge, hunt, and perform within ornate environments that feel half myth, half party, half trap. The ambiguity is the point: the image refuses a single moral or narrative key.

## Why The Work Is Good

The paintings are good because their excess is controlled. Pattern, costume, foliage, animal traits, and acidic color could collapse into decorative noise, but Makinson keeps them tense through choreography. Bodies tilt at strange angles, glances ricochet, spaces buckle, and every ornamental surface seems to participate in the scene's psychology. Her characters are active rather than displayed; they possess appetite, menace, humor, and secrecy. The pleasure of looking is continually interrupted by the sense that the figures know more than the viewer does.

## Key Works

Important works and exhibitions include *Skin Spy* (2020), *Wild Cherry Sphinx* (2018), *The Chief Leaf* (2018), *Trickster Chic* (2018), *This is going to be a tense afternoon* (2021), *Come on, lets find another nightmare* (2021), *And other darlings* (2021), and the exhibition *Stay here while I get a curse* at Lyles & King in 2021. Recent works such as *Other Architects* and *School* continue her interest in staged social worlds and unstable group dynamics.

## Principles I'll Carry

Let beauty behave suspiciously. Use ornament as a carrier of plot, not as filler. Build scenes where each figure has private intention. Allow color to seduce first and complicate second. Treat fantasy as a way to reveal power structures, not to avoid them. Keep the viewer just outside the circle of knowledge.

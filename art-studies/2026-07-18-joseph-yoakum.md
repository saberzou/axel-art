# Joseph Yoakum (1888–1972) — Outsider Landscape

## Who They Are
Joseph Elmer Yoakum was an American self-taught "visionary" landscapist of African-American and Native descent whose life reads like tall-tale folklore: a nomadic youth spent riding the rails, traveling with circuses, stowing away on ships, and serving as a soldier in WWI. He didn't begin drawing seriously until the 1950s, and only in the last decade of his life — roughly 1962–1972 — did he draw obsessively, producing over 2,000 landscapes, sometimes several a day. He said the work came to him by "spiritual unfoldment."

## Core Insight
Landscape as *memory reconstructed*, not observed. Yoakum drew mountains, rivers, and valleys of real places (Mount Cloubelle Jamaica, the Rockies, distant continents) entirely from imagination and recollection. The truth of the place matters less than the felt sensation of terrain — the swell, fold, and flow of the earth as it lived in his mind.

## Why The Work Is Good
His signature is **striation**: hills and mountains rendered as stacked, rippling contour bands, like topographic lines made emotional. The land breathes — ridges undulate like muscle, water pools in soft lobes, trees cluster as rhythmic marks. He used ballpoint, colored pencil, and pastel in muted, chalky earth tones. Every form is outlined and interior-filled with parallel strokes, giving the whole surface a woven, pulsing quality. It's spare yet dense, naive yet utterly assured in its internal logic.

## Key Works
- *Mowich Lake in Cascade Mountain Range near Enumclaw Washington*
- *Mt. Cloubelle of Jamaica West Indies*
- Countless "This is..." titled mountain-and-water scenes, each labeled in his own cursive.

## Principles I'll Carry
- **Contour as feeling** — describe form with flowing parallel bands, not shading.
- **Memory over accuracy** — invent the place; honor the sensation.
- **Muted earth palette** — chalky ochres, dusty greens, soft blues.
- **Everything outlined, everything filled** — no empty passivity; every region has its own rhythm.
- **Undulation** — let land ripple and fold like a living body.

# Peter Doig — Study (2026-07-02)

## Who They Are
Peter Doig (b. 1959, Edinburgh) is a Scottish-born painter who spent his childhood in Trinidad and Canada, trained in London, and eventually returned to Trinidad to live and work. He emerged in the early 1990s as one of the most influential figurative painters of his generation, credited with reviving ambitious landscape painting at a moment when the medium was declared exhausted. His large canvases command record prices, but his real legacy is a whole generation of younger painters who now blend representation, abstraction, and personal memory in his wake.

## Core Insight
**Paint the memory, not the scene.** Doig never paints from life. He works from found images — film stills, album covers, magazine clippings, snapshots — layered on top of his own recollections, until the source dissolves and what remains is the *feeling* of a place rather than its record. The image is a trigger; the painting is what memory does to it afterward. Nostalgia, unease, and the half-remembered coexist in a single frame.

## Why The Work Is Good
The paintings are quietly destabilizing. A calm surface hides an undertow: *Canoe Lake* looks serene until you register the dread borrowed from a horror film. He builds space that is fully visible yet physically inaccessible — you can see into the ravine or across the lake, but you can never enter. Surface handling does the emotional work: dripped, scumbled, and stained passages (echoing Munch and the Canadian Group of Seven) turn generic scenes into something more ardent and desirable than the viewer's own memories. Reality and artifice blur on purpose.

## Key Works
- **Canoe Lake / Swamped (1990s)** — serene reflection charged with cinematic dread.
- **The Architect's Home in the Ravine (1991)** — a modernist house buried in a dense, Pollock-like tangle of branches; visible but unreachable.
- **White Canoe (1990–91)** — the reflected-water motif that made his name.
- **At the Edge of Town** — mood over description; memory as atmosphere.

## Principles I'll Carry
1. **Work from images, then let memory corrupt them.** The distortion is the art.
2. **Visible but inaccessible.** Build depth the viewer can see into but never enter.
3. **Bury the unease.** Let a calm surface carry a second, darker reading underneath.
4. **Let the surface feel.** Drips, stains, and scumbles carry emotion that clean rendering kills.

# Hayv Kahraman

## Who They Are
Hayv Kahraman (b. 1981, Baghdad) is a Kurdish-Iraqi-American painter who fled Iraq for Sweden during the Gulf War, studied in Florence, and now works in Los Angeles. Her practice addresses racialized gender, body politics, migrant consciousness, and the marginal spaces of diasporic life. She calls the work semi-autobiographical.

## Core Insight
The migrant body is a body **separated from itself**. Kahraman's central image is fragmentation — limbs detached, reassembled, cataloged — as the literal visual grammar of exile. The refugee doesn't just lose a place; they lose the coherence of their own body and identity, and are asked to reassemble it on demand to prove they belong.

## Why The Work Is Good
She fuses seemingly incompatible visual traditions — Italian Renaissance figuration, Japanese woodblock line, Iraqi/Islamic ornament and manuscript design — into a single controlled surface. The technical restraint (clean contour, flat elegant color, pale grounds) makes the violence of the subject land harder: serene beauty carrying trauma. Repetition of one recurring female figure turns a personal face into a system, a "kit," a diagram of displacement.

## Key Works
- **How Iraqi Are You** (2015) — women violently detach each other's limbs.
- **Iraqi Kit** (2016) — the same body parts neatly assembled as a modular "build-your-own" set.
- Ongoing series weaving Mahaba/love poetry, mosque-lattice patterning, and woven canvas (literal cuts and weaves in the surface).

## Principles I'll Carry
1. **Let beauty carry the wound.** Elegant, calm execution can make hard content hit harder than shock does.
2. **Fragmentation is a language.** Detaching, cataloging, and reassembling a body/object is a way to make identity legible as a diagram.
3. **Fuse incompatible traditions on one surface** — Renaissance figure + ornament + flat line — so the collision itself becomes meaning.
4. **Repeat one figure into a system** so the personal reads as structural.
5. **Restraint of palette and line** lets emotional weight, not color noise, do the work.

# Louis Fratino — Artist Study

## Who They Are

Louis Fratino (born 1993 in Annapolis, Maryland) is an American painter based in New York. He earned a BFA from the Maryland Institute College of Art in 2015 and later studied in Berlin on a Fulbright. Fratino paints the people and places nearest to him: lovers, relatives, bedrooms, kitchens, city streets, and remembered landscapes. His work brings queer intimacy into conversation with modernism and appeared in the 60th Venice Biennale in 2024.

## Core Insight

Fratino's central discovery is that ordinary life becomes monumental when attention is both loving and formally adventurous. Memory is not merely a source of subject matter; it is a useful distortion mechanism. A remembered room or body can be compressed, tilted, enlarged, or recolored until emotional importance determines scale. Art history enters the same way: not as quotation for its own sake, but as material absorbed and transformed by lived experience.

## Why The Work Is Good

The paintings hold tenderness and erotic charge without separating either from domestic routine. A sink of dishes, a sleeping couple, or a cluttered table receives the same pictorial seriousness as a nude. High-contrast color, elastic anatomy, shallow space, and tactile surfaces make bodies and objects feel present. Limbs bend to fit the frame, perspectives collide, and repeated curves bind figures to furniture. That pressure gives small experiences unusual weight. Modernist devices become a living language for queer companionship, desire, solitude, and family life.

## Key Works

- *Tom* (2019) — an intimate portrait that condenses likeness into sculptural shape and color.
- *Self-Portrait* (2019) — direct self-scrutiny filtered through modernist construction.
- *The Sleepers* (2020) — humor and tenderness carried by exaggerated perspective and partially hidden bodies.
- *Eggs, Dishes, Coreopsis* (2020) — a mundane sink transformed into a dense domestic still life.
- *Richardson Street living room* (2020) — memory, habitation, and compressed interior space.
- *Wine* (2024) — large-scale conviviality organized through saturated color and bodily rhythm.

## Principles I'll Carry

1. Let emotional importance, not optics, determine scale and perspective.
2. Treat domestic residue as evidence of affection and lived time.
3. Filter references through memory until they become personal rather than borrowed.
4. Use color structurally to connect bodies, objects, and atmosphere.
5. Make intimacy specific enough to become universal.

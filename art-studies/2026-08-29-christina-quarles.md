# Christina Quarles

## Who They Are

Christina Quarles, born in 1985, is a Los Angeles-based contemporary painter known for charged figurative works that refuse fixed readings of body, identity, gender, race, and space. Born in Chicago and raised in Los Angeles, she built a deep drawing practice early, later studying art and philosophy at Hampshire College and earning an MFA in painting from Yale. Her work has appeared in major institutional contexts including the Hammer Museum's Made in L.A., the Venice Biennale, MCA Chicago, Hamburger Bahnhof, and public collections such as the Whitney, Tate, Walker Art Center, and PAMM.

## Core Insight

Quarles treats the body as something lived from within but constantly misread from outside. Her figures bend, interlock, multiply, and press against patterned grounds, architectural frames, and impossible planes. The point is not distortion for spectacle; it is ambiguity as truth. A body can be intimate and illegible at once. Space can hold you, classify you, and fail to contain you.

## Why The Work Is Good

The paintings work because Quarles joins rigorous draftsmanship with instability. Limbs are credible enough to feel weighted, warm, and vulnerable, but their boundaries slip into abstraction, furniture, shadow, and decorative pattern. The compositions often use bright color, clipped spatial zones, and graphic structures that feel almost digital, yet the paint remains fleshy and searching. That tension keeps the viewer active: you cannot consume the figure passively, because the figure keeps reorganizing the terms of looking.

## Key Works

Important works and bodies of work include "Forced Perspective (And I Kno It's Rigged, But It's tha Only Game in Town)" from 2018, "Casually Cruel" from 2018, "Feel'd" from 2018, "Laid Down Beside Yew" from 2019, and "Held Fast and Let Go Likewise" from 2020. Her exhibition "The Ground Glows Black" extends the work through the psychic aftermath of the 2025 Los Angeles fires, pushing toward darker, more displaced emotional territory.

## Principles I'll Carry

Let bodies exceed labels. Use patterned space as pressure, not decoration. Make anatomy convincing enough that its impossible transformations matter. Allow ambiguity to be a form of precision. Build images where identity is not a caption attached to a figure, but a shifting relationship between figure, ground, gaze, gravity, and constraint.

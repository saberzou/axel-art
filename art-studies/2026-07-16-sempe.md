# Sempé (Jean-Jacques Sempé)

**Category:** Illustration / Line
**Studied:** 2026-07-16

## Who They Are
Jean-Jacques Sempé (1932–2022) was a French illustrator and cartoonist, largely self-taught, who rose from a difficult childhood in Bordeaux to become one of the most beloved graphic humorists of the 20th century. He co-created the *Le Petit Nicolas* books with René Goscinny, published some forty albums of drawings beginning with *Rien n'est simple* (1962), and was one of the very few French artists to draw covers for *The New Yorker*, a relationship that began in the 1970s.

## Core Insight
Scale carries the feeling. Sempé draws people tiny and the world enormous — a lone cyclist under a cathedral of trees, one open window lit among a hundred dark ones. The gap between the small figure and the vast space *is* the emotion: solitude, wonder, gentle absurdity, tenderness. He doesn't illustrate the joke; he stages the human being inside a world too big for them, and lets us feel our own smallness with affection instead of dread.

## Why The Work Is Good
- **The line.** A delicate, spidery, almost hesitant pen line that looks effortless but hits its distinctive character early and never wavers. Nothing is over-rendered.
- **White space as subject.** Empty paper isn't background — it's air, distance, silence. He trusts the void to do emotional work.
- **Gray washes.** Restrained watercolor grays (and later color) give atmosphere — weather, light, mood — without ever crowding the drawing.
- **Long perspectives.** Deep receding vistas make the viewer feel the size of the room, the street, the sky.
- **Tender irony.** The humor is never cruel. He mocks human vanity and loneliness while clearly loving the humans.

## Key Works
- *Le Petit Nicolas* series (with Goscinny)
- *Rien n'est simple* (1962) and ~40 subsequent albums
- Numerous *New Yorker* covers (1970s–2010s)
- *The Joys and Torments of Solitude*; *Morning Music*

## Principles I'll Carry
1. **Let scale do the emotional work** — a small figure in a large frame says more than a big figure filling it.
2. **Trust empty space.** Don't fill every corner; silence is content.
3. **One confident, unfussy line** beats heavy rendering.
4. **Be tender, not cruel** — observe human smallness with warmth.
5. **Atmosphere via restraint** — a few gray washes, not a flood of color.

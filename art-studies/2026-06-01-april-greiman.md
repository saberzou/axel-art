# April Greiman — Study Notes (2026-06-01)

## Who They Are
April Greiman (b. 1948, New York) is an American graphic designer often credited as one of the first designers to fully embrace the Macintosh and digital tools as legitimate art-making instruments. Trained in Switzerland under Wolfgang Weingart and Armin Hofmann, she carried Swiss typographic rigor to California in the late 1970s, where it collided with West Coast looseness to produce what came to be called the "New Wave." She is best known for her 1986 *Design Quarterly 133* issue — "Does It Make Sense?" — a single fold-out poster of her own life-size digital self-portrait, assembled from low-resolution MacPaint bitmaps. AIGA Medalist (1998), AGI member, principal of Made in Space, Los Angeles.

## Core Insight
**"Everything is an object in space."** Greiman treats the page (or screen) as a three-dimensional field where photography, type, video stills, hand-drawn marks and computer pixels all coexist as equal citizens. She does not hide the seams of her tools — bitmaps stay jagged, video stays scanned, type floats and rotates — because the *hybrid* itself is the message. Texture, layer, and the residue of process are content, not noise.

## Why The Work Is Good
It is good because she refused the false choice between Swiss discipline and digital chaos. The grid is still there — you can feel it under every collage — but it has been tilted, exploded into z-space, and populated with materials no one else considered "designable" yet (early color Mac screenshots, fax artifacts, ultrasound scans). The work looks like the future arriving in real time, while staying compositionally readable. She made low resolution feel luxurious.

## Key Works
- *Does It Make Sense?* — Design Quarterly 133 (1986), 2×6 ft digital self-portrait poster.
- Pacific Design Center identity and signage (1980s–90s).
- *Wet* magazine layouts (early 80s, with Jayme Odgers).
- "Iris Light" prints and large-scale architectural color work (Made in Space studio).

## Principles I'll Carry
1. **Trust the artifact.** Pixels, scan lines, JPEG mush — leave the fingerprint of the tool visible.
2. **Layer with hierarchy, not noise.** Many materials can share one frame if scale, value, and grid still do their job underneath.
3. **Z-axis the page.** Treat 2D surfaces as space, not as flat real estate.
4. **Hybrid is a position.** Mixing media is not indecision; it is an argument that the boundary itself is obsolete.

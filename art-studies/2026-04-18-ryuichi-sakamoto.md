# Ryuichi Sakamoto (1952–2023)

**Category:** Film Scoring / Electronic Composition

## Who He Was

Japanese composer, pianist, producer, activist. Born in Tokyo, classically trained from age three, studied electronic music and ethnomusicology at Tokyo University of the Arts. Co-founded Yellow Magic Orchestra (YMO) in 1978, which pioneered synth-pop and electronic music globally. Acted in and scored *Merry Christmas, Mr. Lawrence* (1983). Won an Oscar, Grammy, and BAFTA for *The Last Emperor* (1987). Spent four decades dissolving the walls between electronic, classical, ambient, noise, and world music. Died of cancer in March 2023.

## Philosophy

Sakamoto believed all musical traditions stand on equal footing. There is no hierarchy between a Bach fugue and a gamelan pattern, between synthesizer noise and a solo cello. He treated genre categories the way a polyglot treats languages: useful containers, never prisons.

His deepest conviction, especially in his late work, was that sound exists before music. Rain on a window, the hum of a hospital ventilator, the creak of a tsunami-damaged piano. These aren't raw material waiting to be elevated into composition. They're already complete. The composer's job is to listen first, impose second.

After surviving throat cancer in 2014, this idea sharpened into something almost spiritual. His album *async* (2017) imagined a film soundtrack for a movie by Andrei Tarkovsky that never existed. It's built from field recordings, organ drones, electronic glitches, and silence treated as a structural element. The pieces don't resolve. They breathe unevenly, like a person sleeping.

## What Makes His Work Good

**Emotional precision through restraint.** The "Merry Christmas Mr. Lawrence" theme uses a handful of notes and a simple arpeggio pattern. It shouldn't be devastating, but it is. Sakamoto understood that complexity is not the same as depth. A single held chord can carry more weight than a full orchestral arrangement if you place it at exactly the right moment.

**Refusal to repeat himself.** From YMO's playful technopop to *Beauty*'s Okinawan folk fusion to *BTTB*'s solo piano minimalism to *async*'s ambient deconstruction, each phase is a genuine reinvention. He didn't evolve linearly; he moved laterally, picking up and discarding tools as curiosity demanded.

**Sound as material, not decoration.** In his later installations with Shiro Takatani, and throughout *async* and *12* (his final album, 2023), Sakamoto treated sonic texture the way a sculptor treats clay. The grain of a recording, the room tone, the artifacts of digital processing. These weren't imperfections to clean up but surfaces to explore.

**Time as the real medium.** His late work especially understands that music's deepest power is its relationship with duration. *12* was composed in fragments during his cancer treatment, each piece dated like a diary entry. The album doesn't build toward anything. It exists in a present tense that acknowledges its own impermanence.

## Core Principles

1. **Listen before you compose.** The world is already full of music. Your job is to notice it.
2. **All traditions are equal.** Hierarchies between genres, between Western and non-Western, between acoustic and electronic, are artificial. Ignore them.
3. **Imperfection is information.** A detuned piano, a digital glitch, a cracked voice. These carry meaning that perfection erases.
4. **Restraint amplifies emotion.** The note you don't play matters as much as the one you do.
5. **Every phase is complete.** Don't cling to what worked before. Each new work starts from zero.

## Key Works

- **Merry Christmas, Mr. Lawrence** (1983) — the theme that proved electronic sensibility could produce devastating emotional clarity
- **The Last Emperor** (1987) — Oscar-winning score blending Chinese, Japanese, and Western orchestral traditions
- **Beauty** (1989) — Okinawan and Balinese music fused with electronic production; a landmark in world-meets-electronic
- **BTTB (Back to the Basics)** (1998) — stripped-back solo piano, Satie-adjacent minimalism
- **async** (2017) — post-cancer masterwork; imaginary Tarkovsky soundtrack built from found sound and silence
- **12** (2023) — final album, diary-like fragments composed during cancer treatment; each track dated, none trying to be more than what it is

## The Core Insight

Sound is already music before a composer touches it. The greatest act of composition is knowing when to get out of the way. Sakamoto's career is a forty-year demonstration that curiosity matters more than mastery, that vulnerability sounds better than virtuosity, and that the most honest music admits it will end.

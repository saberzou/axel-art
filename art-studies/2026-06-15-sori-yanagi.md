# Sori Yanagi — Nightly Artist Study (2026-06-15)

## Who They Are
Sori Yanagi (1915–2011) was a Japanese industrial designer and the son of Soetsu Yanagi, founder of the Mingei (folk craft) movement. He bridged two worlds that usually fight each other: the warmth of handcraft and the cold efficiency of industrial mass-production. His most famous object, the **Butterfly Stool** (1954), won the Gold Medal at the 1957 Milan Triennale — two molded plywood wings joined by a single brass rod, evoking both a butterfly and a calligraphic stroke. He designed everyday tools sold in kitchen shops, not museum vitrines: kettles, ladles, cutlery, white porcelain.

## Core Insight
**Good form is discovered with the hands, not drawn on paper.** Yanagi refused to design from technical sketches. He shaped objects in plaster and clay models, refining them by touch until the form felt right in the palm, then reverse-engineered the manufacturing. Beauty, for him, emerged from use and the body — not from a designer's signature.

## Why The Work Is Good
The work disappears into daily life. His kettle (1994, made in Niigata across 15+ factories) and his stainless ladles are objects you reach for without thinking — the handle simply fits. He proved that mass-produced goods could carry the soul of Mingei: anonymous, honest, made for ordinary people. He "designed for use, not for show" (unlike contemporaries collected only by the rich). The restraint is total: no decoration, only the irreducible essential form.

## Key Works
- **Butterfly Stool** (1954) — molded plywood, brass rod
- **Stainless Steel Kettle** (1994, Niigata)
- **Stainless cutlery & kitchen tools** — ladles, bowls, the everyday range
- **White porcelain tableware** — Mingei warmth at industrial scale
- **Tokyo Olympic torch holder** (1964)

## Principles I'll Carry
1. **Resolve form by touch, not by sketch** — let the hand and the body judge.
2. **Anonymous beauty** — the best object hides the designer; it serves, it doesn't perform.
3. **Honor the essential** — strip until only the necessary form remains.
4. **Marry warmth and precision** — handcraft soul inside industrial discipline.
5. **Design for use, not for show.**

# Hilma af Klint — Study (2026-07-04)

**Category:** Painting / Abstraction

## Who They Are
Hilma af Klint (1862–1944) was a Swedish painter who produced sweeping abstract compositions from around 1906 — years before Kandinsky, Mondrian, or Malevich, the men usually credited with inventing abstraction. Trained conventionally at Stockholm's Royal Academy, she earned a living painting botanical illustrations and portraits, but her real work was hidden. As part of a group of five women called "De Fem" (The Five), she held séances and made automatic drawings, and in 1906 began *The Paintings for the Temple* — 193 works over roughly a decade, meant to hang in a spiral temple that was never built. She showed the abstract work almost never in her lifetime and stipulated it stay hidden until 20 years after her death. The world only truly met it in 2018.

## Core Insight
Abstraction did not have to be about *form for form's sake* — it could be a **diagram of the invisible**. Af Klint treated painting as a way to make unseen forces (spiritual evolution, the unity beneath dualism, the structure of growth) visible through a private but rigorous symbolic language.

## Why The Work Is Good
The paintings feel *systematic*, not decorative. She built a consistent grammar: spirals and snail shells for evolution and development; blue for the female principle and yellow for the male (drawn from Goethe's *Theory of Colours*), with green as their union; the egg for origin; lilies, roses, eyelets and hooks for gendered polarity. Botanical precision from her illustration training keeps even the wildest mandalas anchored and legible. *The Ten Largest* (1907) — huge, joyous, tempera on paper — move from childhood to old age in pinks, oranges, and blues, full of loops and seashell curls. The scale, confidence, and internal logic are what make it hold up: it reads as a coherent cosmology, not improvisation.

## Key Works
- **The Ten Largest** (1907) — the four ages of life, monumental and radiant.
- **Paintings for the Temple** (1906–1915) — the 193-work spiritual cycle.
- **The Swan (SUW) series** (1914–15) — black/white swans resolving into pure geometry.
- **The Dove series** — figurative-to-abstract spiritual struggle.
- **Primordial Chaos (WU/Rosen)** — small early abstractions of the world's origin.

## Principles I'll Carry
1. **Build a symbol system, then trust it.** Consistency of vocabulary makes abstraction legible.
2. **Let color carry meaning, not just mood** — assign it a role and hold the assignment.
3. **Anchor the mystical in the observed** — her botanical rigor keeps the visionary from going mushy.
4. **The spiral = growth**: motion and directionality can encode an idea, not just fill space.
5. **Make the invisible diagrammatic** — paint the structure behind a thing, not its surface.

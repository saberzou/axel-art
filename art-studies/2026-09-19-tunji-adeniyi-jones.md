# Tunji Adeniyi-Jones — Artist Study

## Who They Are

Tunji Adeniyi-Jones (born 1992, London) is a British-Nigerian painter and printmaker based in New York. He builds a figurative language from Yoruba heritage, West African mythology, Nigerian modernism, the Harlem Renaissance, and European abstraction. His figures are often androgynous silhouettes suspended within compressed fields of foliage and pattern.

## Core Insight

The body can be both culturally specific and radically open: a carrier of ancestry, migration, ceremony, desire, and imagined possibility. Adeniyi-Jones turns this idea into “chromatic space.” Figure and environment do not sit in front of one another; their curves, colors, and repetitions interlock until the entire surface feels choreographed. Movement becomes a form of memory. He often rotates the canvas while working, removing a stable horizon so that bodies can float, dive, and turn without obeying ordinary gravity.

## Why The Work Is Good

The paintings reconcile opposites. Monumental bodies feel buoyant; shallow space becomes energetic; precise drawing supports ecstatic color. Repetition—paired figures, echoed limbs, recurring leaves—suggests ritual rather than decoration. Silhouettes invite identification without fixing identity, while eyes restore agency and direct the viewer through the composition. Yoruba and diasporic histories are not ornaments applied to Western modernism, but active systems that reorganize the picture.

## Key Works

- **Poetic Feet I–III** — paired dancers move through flame-like red and orange patterning; doubling connects ceremony, transformation, and the artist’s imagined childhood siblings.
- **Twin Virtues** — interlocking horizontal figures evoke Yoruba reverence for twins while turning bodies into an undulating landscape.
- **Five Virtues** — five gesturing figures embody moral attributes, linking sacred imagery, self-identification, and transcultural memory.
- **Midnight Voices** — lithographs of reclining silhouettes among dense flora; intimate, unpredictable studies that fed the larger paintings in *Deep Dive*.
- **A Flashy Encounter** — a looser work on paper that brings Harlem Renaissance illustration, fluid material behavior, and diasporic histories into one charged meeting.

## Principles I'll Carry

1. Let figure and ground share one rhythm rather than separating subject from setting.
2. Use repetition as ceremony: each recurrence should deepen meaning.
3. Build motion through contour, gaze, and rotated orientation—not literal action alone.
4. Treat color as a spatial force and emotional climate.
5. Translate cultural memory into a living visual system, never a decorative quotation.

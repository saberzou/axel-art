# Etel Adnan — 2026-06-25

**Category:** Painting / Poetry

## Who They Are
Etel Adnan (1925–2021) was a Lebanese-American painter, poet, and essayist born in Beirut to a Greek mother and Syrian father, raised across Arabic, Greek, Turkish, and French. She came to painting late and almost by accident in the late 1950s while teaching philosophy in California, and it became the language she'd been missing. She lived between Beirut, Paris, and Sausalito, and her small, radiant oil paintings — most no bigger than a notebook — are now in M+, the Tate, and the Met. She was a writer first (the war novel *Sitt Marie Rose* is a classic), and the painting and the poetry never stopped feeding each other.

## Core Insight
Color is a language you can speak when words fail or betray you. Conflicted about writing in French — the colonizer's tongue — Adnan said abstraction let her "paint in Arabic": *"I didn't need to use words, but colors and lines."* The painting isn't decoration of an idea; it **is** the thought, made of pigment instead of grammar. "My spirit is anguished by colour."

## Why The Work Is Good
She works almost entirely with a **palette knife**, laying down small, confident blocks of saturated, unmixed color — a red sun, an ochre slope, a blue field — with no fuss, no blending, no hesitation. The economy is the whole point: a mountain becomes three flat shapes and somehow holds more presence than a photorealistic rendering. The paintings feel *certain*. There's no anxiety in them, only directness — joy as a discipline, made by someone who lived through Lebanon's civil war and chose clarity anyway.

## Key Works
- **Mount Tamalpais series** (1960s–2010s) — the California peak painted obsessively for decades as a recurring "consciousness," each version a fresh meditation.
- **Leporellos** (1964–) — accordion-fold artist's books fusing painting, Arabic calligraphy, and poetry into one unbroken horizontal river.
- ***Journey to Mount Tamalpais*** (1986) — her book on nature, color, and seeing.
- Early architectural abstractions (1960–64) — ochre/red staggered bands, before the mountain took over.

## Principles I'll Carry
1. **Unmixed color, placed with conviction** — flat blocks, no blending, no apology.
2. **Reduce a subject to a few shapes** — fewer marks, more presence.
3. **Let the medium be the thought**, not a translation of one.
4. **Format as meaning** — the long horizontal scroll *is* the journey.
5. **Choose clarity and joy** even when the source is hard.

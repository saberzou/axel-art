# Amy Sherald

## Who They Are

Amy Sherald is an American painter born in Columbus, Georgia in 1973, now based around the New York City area. She is best known for large, carefully staged portraits of Black Americans, including the official National Portrait Gallery portrait of Michelle Obama, the Vanity Fair portrait of Breonna Taylor, and major works gathered in the traveling retrospective *Amy Sherald: American Sublime*. Her paintings often begin with selected sitters, clothing, props, and photographs, but the final images are not documentary records. They are composed fictions that give ordinary presence a heightened, emblematic force.

## Core Insight

Sherald's central move is to separate identity from easy visual coding. Her gray-toned skin interrupts habitual readings of race while brilliant clothing, flat color fields, and direct gazes rebuild the sitter as a sovereign presence. The person is specific, but the scene feels suspended outside ordinary time.

## Why The Work Is Good

The paintings are restrained and theatrical at once. Sherald does not overload the canvas with setting; she lets posture, silhouette, wardrobe, and color carry the narrative. This makes the portraits instantly legible from a distance, then slower and stranger up close. Her figures often seem calm rather than passive: they meet the viewer with self-possession, not performance. The color is especially powerful because it is declarative without becoming noisy. A pale ground, a saturated dress, a patterned shirt, or a bicycle can do the work that atmosphere might do in another painter's hand.

Sherald also understands that representation can be spacious. She paints Black American life without reducing it to trauma, protest, or biography, while still allowing history to press against the image. Leisure, style, pause, and interiority become serious subjects.

## Key Works

Important works include *Miss Everything (Unsuppressed Deliverance)*, which won the National Portrait Gallery's Outwin Boochever Portrait Competition in 2016; *Michelle LaVaughn Robinson Obama*; *The Bathers*; *Portrait of Breonna Taylor*; *For Love, and for Country*; *A Midsummer Afternoon Dream*; *Kingdom*; and *Trans Forming Liberty*.

## Principles I'll Carry

Use restraint as a way to intensify presence. Let color function like architecture. Build a figure through stance, gaze, and chosen objects rather than explanatory context. Make ordinary life feel iconic without freezing it into propaganda. Leave room around the subject so the viewer has to confront the person, not just the scene.

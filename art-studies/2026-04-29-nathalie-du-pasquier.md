# Nathalie Du Pasquier — Pattern Design / Surface Art

**Born:** 1957, Bordeaux, France
**Based:** Milan, since 1979
**Category:** Pattern Design / Surface Art

## Who She Is

Nathalie Du Pasquier is a French-born artist and designer who discovered pattern and texture while traveling through West Africa in the 1970s. She landed in Milan at 22 and became a founding member of the Memphis Group in 1981, Ettore Sottsass's radical collective that blew up the beige consensus of "good taste" modernism. For Memphis she designed textiles, carpets, plastic laminates, furniture. Her patterns were explosive: clashing geometries, aggressive color, zero apology.

Then in 1987 she walked away from design entirely and dedicated herself to painting. Still lifes. For 35+ years.

That pivot is the key to understanding her.

## Philosophy

Du Pasquier's core insight: **pattern is condensed thought**. In her own words, "designing pattern is a way to condense a state of mind, memory, aspirations and more without having to be too clever." Pattern doesn't narrate. It resonates. It carries information below the threshold of language.

Her second insight is about the permeability between dimensions. She's spent decades investigating the relationship between objects and the spaces they occupy, constantly moving between 2D and 3D, between representation and abstraction, between a painted cup and the actual cup. Her still lifes aren't about the objects. They're about how objects generate space around themselves.

She said: "From the beginning, I did not belong to the design world or to the art world." This is not false modesty. It's her operating principle. Categories are walls. She walks through them.

## Why the Work is Good

1. **Decoration as intelligence.** Memphis took ornament (dismissed since Loos's "Ornament and Crime") and treated it as a vehicle for ideas. Du Pasquier's patterns aren't decorative in the pejorative sense. They're systems of visual logic operating at full intensity. Every surface becomes an argument.

2. **The courage to be obvious.** Her color is loud. Her geometry is blunt. There's no ironic distance, no safe intellectual framing. She commits to the visual event completely. This is harder than it looks. Most artists hedge.

3. **Continuity through transformation.** The patterns became paintings. The paintings reference objects. The objects become sculptures. She described it as "a chain of thoughts that follow each other." Nothing is abandoned, only translated.

4. **Autonomy of the painted object.** Her still lifes don't depict real arrangements. She invents impossible spatial relationships, stacks objects that couldn't support each other, uses colors that flatten depth deliberately. The painting is the reality, not a window onto one.

## Key Works

- **Memphis textiles and laminates** (1981–1986) — patterns like "Gabon," "Zambia," "Sudan," drawing on African textile traditions but exploding them into postmodern geometries
- **10 Tappeti Moderni (10 Modern Carpets)** (1986, with George Sowden) — rugs as autonomous graphic statements
- **Still life paintings** (1987–present) — increasingly complex compositions where objects, shadows, and spaces negotiate impossible coexistence
- **"Other Rooms"** at Camden Arts Centre (2017) — immersive installation blurring painting, sculpture, and architectural space
- **HAY collaboration** (2016–) — patterns returned to functional objects, closing the loop between her design and painting lives
- **"domino"** (2002) — artist book exploring the seriality of pattern as a form of thinking

## Core Principles

- **Surface is not superficial.** What's on the outside carries as much meaning as what's "inside."
- **Pattern as pre-verbal language.** Geometric relationships communicate before words arrive.
- **Refuse categories.** Art, design, craft, decoration — these are filing systems, not creative realities.
- **Commit fully.** Color at full saturation. Shape at full boldness. No safety net of subtlety.
- **Everything connects.** Each body of work feeds the next. Nothing is a dead end.

# Kerry James Marshall — Study (2026-07-06)

## Who They Are
Kerry James Marshall (b. October 17, 1955, Birmingham, Alabama) is an American painter widely regarded as one of the most important figurative artists alive. He grew up amid the Civil Rights era and the Watts uprising in Los Angeles, later teaching painting at the University of Illinois at Chicago. In 2017 he made the Time 100. He is best known for large-scale paintings of Black figures rendered in an emphatic, near-absolute black — a body of work that culminated in the acclaimed 2016 retrospective *Mastry* (MCA Chicago / Met Breuer / MOCA LA).

## Core Insight
The Western art-historical canon is overwhelmingly populated by white bodies; Black subjects, when present, were servants, exotics, or absent. Marshall's answer is not protest but *insertion*: paint Black figures with the full grandeur, technical rigor, and everyday normalcy the canon reserved for others. Blackness becomes the subject, the palette, and the assertion — a deliberate reclamation of the word and the color.

## Why The Work Is Good
Three strategies interlock. (1) **Radical black skin** — he mixes carbon, mars, and ivory blacks so faces read as unapologetically, luminously Black rather than "brown." It forces the eye to slow down and truly *see*, echoing Ralph Ellison's *Invisible Man*: "who refuses to see people like me, and why?" (2) **Painterly mastery** — he pursued representational virtuosity precisely when it was unfashionable, so no one could dismiss the work as merely political. (3) **Normalcy as dignity** — portraits, landscapes, barbershops, domestic interiors, lovers in a park: Black life shown as utterly, beautifully ordinary.

## Key Works
- *A Portrait of the Artist as a Shadow of His Former Self* (1980) — tiny 8×6.5" breakthrough; a black-hatted Black man dissolving into a dark ground, gap-toothed grin and eyes floating.
- *De Style* (1993) — a barbershop scene whose title puns on De Stijl/Mondrian, wedging Black community into a white modernist lineage.
- *Past Times* (1997) — leisure-scape of Black figures boating and picnicking; sold for $21.1M in 2018.
- *School of Beauty, School of Culture* (2012) — a salon with a warped Disney-princess anamorphosis critiquing beauty standards.

## Principles I'll Carry
- Reclamation over apology: turn the thing that was denied into the whole language of the piece.
- Master the conventional craft first — virtuosity earns the right to subvert.
- Dignity lives in the ordinary; grandeur is a choice about *who* gets the epic treatment.
- One controlled extreme (his absolute black) can reorganize how the entire image is read.

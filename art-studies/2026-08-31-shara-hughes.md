# Shara Hughes

## Who They Are

Shara Hughes is an American painter born in Atlanta in 1981 and based in Brooklyn. She studied at the Rhode Island School of Design and Skowhegan, and became widely known for vivid, invented landscapes that helped re-open contemporary landscape painting as a place for psychological pressure, improvisation, and abstract invention. Her work has appeared in major exhibitions including the 2017 Whitney Biennial, and is held by museums including the Met, the Whitney, the Denver Art Museum, and the Smithsonian American Art Museum.

## Core Insight

Hughes treats landscape less as scenery than as an emotional weather system. The mountain, sun, tree, window, river, or moon is not copied from a place; it is discovered through painting. Her canvases often feel like a mind constructing nature in real time, letting each mark provoke the next. The result is pictorial space that is believable without being naturalistic: a world held together by rhythm, color, and pressure rather than conventional perspective.

## Why The Work Is Good

The paintings are generous and unstable at once. Hughes can use candy-bright color, wild contour, decorative pattern, and theatrical light without letting the work collapse into mere exuberance. There is usually a structural intelligence underneath the lushness: foregrounds push like curtains, trees twist into characters, skies flatten into graphic fields, and pockets of deep space suddenly open behind hard-edged marks. That friction between play and control gives the work its charge. Her landscapes remember Fauvism, Symbolism, Surrealism, Hockney, and Burchfield, but they do not feel like quotations; they feel like painting thinking aloud.

## Key Works

Key bodies and exhibitions include her room in the 2017 Whitney Biennial, the landscape-focused works around "Same Space Different Day," "Return of light," "Time Lapsed" at Kunstmuseum Luzern, "Light the Dark," and recent portal/window-themed work in "Inside Outside." Individual works often emphasize suns, paths, tangled foliage, vibrating horizons, and compressed theatrical viewpoints.

## Principles I'll Carry

Begin without over-defining the destination. Let one decision create the conditions for the next. Use color as structure, not decoration. Make space elastic: flat in one zone, deep in another, symbolic where needed. Let the image stay alive by balancing delight with unease, and by allowing the landscape to behave like an interior state.

# Loie Hollowell — Artist Study

## Who They Are

Loie Hollowell (born 1983, raised in Woodland, California) is a New York–based painter whose work occupies the threshold between geometric abstraction, bodily figuration, and low-relief sculpture. After a BFA at UC Santa Barbara and an MFA in painting at Virginia Commonwealth University, she developed a visual language grounded in autobiographical experiences of sexuality, pregnancy, birth, breastfeeding, and postpartum pain. Her paintings often use bilateral symmetry, a central vertical axis, and symbolic forms such as mandorlas, ogees, orbs, and lingams.

## Core Insight

The body can be translated without being erased. Hollowell compresses private physical sensation into color, geometry, scale, texture, and light, making an intimate event legible as an almost cosmic system. The central insight is not simply that abstract forms can resemble anatomy; it is that formal choices can behave like bodily states. A gradient can throb, concentric rings can contract, symmetry can stabilize or trap, and a bulging surface can make pressure physically present.

## Why The Work Is Good

The work holds opposites in productive tension: explicit and concealed, seductive and painful, precise and vulnerable, flat image and sculptural object. From a distance, the compositions appear controlled and iconic. Up close, textured marks, foam relief, cast forms, and real shadows reveal instability and touch. Hollowell also works at bodily scale, correlating dimensions to heads, breasts, groins, bellies, or full figures. That proportional logic makes the viewer encounter the paintings as presences rather than diagrams. Her strongest works turn autobiography into shared sensation without flattening its specificity.

## Key Works

- **Lingam Twist in Blue and Orange** (2016): an early fusion of sexual symbolism, symmetry, and charged color.
- **Touchy Subject** (2018): relief materials complicate the boundary between painted illusion and physical protrusion.
- **Fully Dilated** (2022): a cast pregnant belly makes birth direct while atmospheric halos retain abstraction.
- **Pressure in Yellow-Blue and Mars Violet** (2022): bodily pain becomes compressed, symmetrical force.
- **Split Orbs** and **Overview Effect** series: concentric pulses and paired orbs translate childbirth into shifting bodily and cosmic scales.

## Principles I'll Carry

1. Translate sensation into formal behavior, not literal illustration.
2. Let real light and shadow participate in the image.
3. Use symmetry as an emotional structure, not mere decoration.
4. Anchor scale to the body so abstraction remains felt.
5. Preserve contradiction: beauty can carry pressure, pain, and vulnerability.

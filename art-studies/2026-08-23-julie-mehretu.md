# Julie Mehretu

## Who They Are
Julie Mehretu is an Ethiopian American painter born in Addis Ababa in 1970, now based in New York. Her large-scale abstract paintings, drawings, and prints build dense fields from architectural plans, maps, diagrams, calligraphic marks, erasures, and atmospheric color. She earned her MFA from RISD in 1997, received a MacArthur Fellowship in 2005, and has been the subject of major museum exhibitions at institutions including LACMA, the Whitney, the Walker, and the Guggenheim.

## Core Insight
Mehretu treats abstraction as a way to hold history in motion. A city, a protest, a market system, a migration route, or a political rupture does not appear as a single image; it appears as a pressure field. Her paintings make visible how architecture, power, memory, and velocity stack over one another until place becomes almost meteorological.

## Why The Work Is Good
The work is powerful because it turns complexity into sensation without simplifying it. Lines behave like maps, flight paths, shrapnel, handwriting, and wind at once. Blurred photographic grounds create a buried political charge, while sharper marks skid, swarm, and collide across the surface. The paintings reward both distant and close viewing: from afar they feel like weather systems or explosions; up close they become choreographies of decision, revision, and residue. Mehretu's strength is not just scale, but orchestration. She lets information overload become rhythm.

## Key Works
Important works include *Retopistics: A Renegade Excavation* (2001), *Empirical Construction, Istanbul* (2003), the *Stadia* series (2004), *Mural* (2009), *Mogamma: A Painting in Four Parts* (2012), *HOWL, eon (I, II)* (2016-2017), and later works such as *Haka (and Riot)* (2019). Across them, civic space becomes a layered psychological and political terrain.

## Principles I'll Carry
Use abstraction to compress many realities into one charged field. Let structure and chaos argue on the same surface. Treat marks as forces, not decoration. Build depth through layers of time rather than perspectival space. Allow erasure, blur, and partial legibility to carry emotional truth. Make the image feel like an event still unfolding.

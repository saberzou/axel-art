# Lee Ufan — Study 2026-06-10

## Who They Are
Lee Ufan (b. 1936, Korea) is a painter, sculptor, philosopher, and poet who became the central theorist of **Mono-ha** ("School of Things"), the late-1960s Japanese movement that turned away from making *new* things and toward staging encounters between existing materials — stone, steel, glass, canvas, air. He's spent six decades making the same argument in different mediums: that art is not about what an artist *adds* to the world, but about what becomes visible when an artist arranges a meeting between materials and lets us *notice* the space around them.

## Core Insight
**The unpainted is not absence — it is the work.** Lee's whole practice is built on the idea that meaning lives in the *relation* between a mark and the void around it, a stone and the steel plate beside it, the body and the breath. He calls his sculptures *Relatum* — "that which relates." Everything is the in-between.

## Why The Work Is Good
Most minimalism feels cold because it strips things away to prove a point. Lee's emptiness is *warm* — it's a Zen-and-phenomenology emptiness that asks you to slow your breathing and notice. The "From Point" and "From Line" paintings (1972–84) are deceptively simple: he loads a brush with mineral pigment and drags it across raw canvas in disciplined, breath-paced repetitions, each stroke fading as the pigment runs out. You can *see* time being used up. You see his body in the rhythm. And the blank canvas around each stroke isn't background — it's the thing the stroke is *encountering*. He sustained this discipline for fifty years without ever decorating it.

## Key Works
- **Relatum (1968– )** — Stone and steel plate compositions. The whole series, retroactively renamed, makes the same point: place two things in a room and the room itself becomes visible.
- **From Point (1972–84)** — Repeated dots of pigment, each fading until the brush is empty, then reloaded. A diagram of how attention dies and is reborn.
- **From Line (1973–84)** — Vertical strokes pulled top to bottom; the line is born saturated and ends as ghost.
- **Dialogue series (2006– )** — One or two enormous brushmarks on a wall-sized canvas. Calligraphic, breath-driven; the late style.

## Principles I'll Carry
1. **The negative space is the subject.** Compose so the emptiness has weight.
2. **One gesture is enough if you mean it.** Don't decorate; don't recover.
3. **Show the body in the mark.** Breath, pressure, fade. Let the medium run out on camera.
4. **Stage an encounter, don't fabricate a thing.** Two elements, a relation between them, and the room.
5. **Discipline over flourish.** Repetition is not boredom — it is how attention deepens.

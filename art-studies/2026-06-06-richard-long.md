# Richard Long — Land Art / Walking

## Who They Are

Richard Long (b. 1945, Bristol, England) is a British sculptor and conceptual artist who, since the late 1960s, has redefined sculpture by making the simple act of walking his primary medium. While still a student at Saint Martin's, he made *A Line Made by Walking* (1967) at age 22 — pacing back and forth across a grass field until his footsteps left a visible line, then photographing it. With that single gesture, he opened a new lineage: land art that is intimate rather than monumental, that leaves almost nothing behind, and that treats the artist's body and time as the real material. He has since walked thousands of miles across Dartmoor, the Andes, the Sahara, Iceland, and Japan, returning with maps, photographs, text works, and stone or driftwood arrangements laid out in galleries.

## Core Insight

**Walking itself is the sculpture.** The line, circle, or photograph is only the residue. The art lives in the embodied passage of a human being through a landscape over time — and in the quiet humility of marking that passage with materials the place itself provides.

## Why The Work Is Good

Because it dissolves the boundary between art-making and being-alive. Long doesn't impose form on nature; he *enters* nature and lets duration, geology, and his own footsteps generate the form. The work is minimal but not cold — every circle of slate carries the memory of a specific river, ridge, weather. It rebukes spectacle: no helicopters, no bulldozers, no permanence. Just one body, one place, one rhythm. That restraint is what makes it feel earned and timeless, closer to a Neolithic cairn than to a contemporary installation.

## Key Works

- **A Line Made by Walking (1967)** — A trampled line in an English field; the founding gesture of walking-as-sculpture.
- **Stone circles & lines** (1970s–present) — Slate, flint, or driftwood arranged on gallery floors, each carrying a specific landscape's geology.
- **A Thousand Miles, A Thousand Hours (1974)** — A walk where the symmetry of the *idea* was the work, leaving no physical trace.
- **Mud hand circles & River Avon mud paintings** — Wall works made by pressing mud-coated hands directly onto the surface.
- **Text walks** — Typed records like "A Walk of Four Hours and Four Circles," where language carries the experience.

## Principles I'll Carry

1. **The process is the piece.** What you do is the work; the artifact is just proof.
2. **Use what the place gives you.** Don't import; gather.
3. **Trust simple geometry.** A line, a circle, a cross — primal shapes hold more than ornament.
4. **Let time do the heavy lifting.** Duration is a material.
5. **Leave lightly.** Restraint is a form of respect.

# David Hockney — Artist Study (2026-06-19)

## Who They Are
David Hockney (b. 1937, Bradford, England) is one of the most influential British painters of the 20th and 21st centuries — a central figure of the Pop Art movement who never stopped reinventing his own eye. From the sun-bleached swimming pools of 1960s Los Angeles (*A Bigger Splash*) to monumental Yorkshire landscapes, photographic "joiners," and late-career iPad paintings, his career is a single long argument about one question: **how do we actually see?**

## Core Insight
Linear, single-point perspective is a lie we inherited from the camera and the Renaissance. Real human vision is **mobile, layered, and lived through time** — we never see a scene from one frozen vanishing point; we scan, turn our heads, accumulate. Hockney's whole project is to put *time and the moving body* back into the still image. The eye wanders; the picture should let it.

## Why The Work Is Good
He refuses to let any medium dictate his vision — he bends the tool to the eye instead. The **photo-collage "joiners"** (early 1980s) assemble dozens of Polaroids or 35mm prints of one subject, each shot from a slightly different angle and moment, into a fractured composite that feels *more* true to perception than a single clean photo. The seams aren't flaws; they're the honest record of a looking body. Later he carried the same restlessness into bright, fearless **iPad paintings** — proving a "lesser" digital tool could hold real painterly ambition. Across every phase: saturated, joyful, un-muddy color; reverse and multiple perspectives; and a refusal to be bored.

## Key Works
- *A Bigger Splash* (1967) — flat California light, the frozen splash against stillness.
- *Pearblossom Hwy.* (1986) — the masterpiece joiner; a desert road built from hundreds of photographs.
- *Mr and Mrs Clark and Percy* (1970–71) — double portrait, cool clarity.
- *The Arrival of Spring* iPad series (2011, 2020) — Woldgate woods, painted on a screen with a finger.

## Principles I'll Carry
1. **Question the frame.** Single-point perspective is a default, not a law — multiple viewpoints can feel truer.
2. **Put time in the still image.** Fragmenting one subject across moments evokes how we really perceive.
3. **The seams are honest.** Visible joints and shifts can carry more truth than seamless polish.
4. **No medium is beneath ambition.** A "humble" tool (iPad, finger) is no excuse for small work.
5. **Color is joy.** Saturation and light are not naive — they're a choice to make seeing feel alive.

# Christina Kimeze — Artist Study

## Who They Are

Christina Kimeze (born 1986) is a British painter based in London. She studied Biological Sciences at Oxford before completing the Royal Drawing School’s Drawing Year in 2022, where she received the Sir Denis Mahon Award. Her practice draws on life, memory, cinema, literature, and Ugandan family history. With pastel, chalk, oil stick, and paint pressed into suede matboard, she builds dreamlike spaces inhabited by women moving between private and shared worlds.

## Core Insight

Kimeze treats interior life as action. Stillness is not emptiness, passivity, or withdrawal; it is a sovereign state where identity can develop beyond the public demand that Black experience always be explained through struggle or resistance. More recently, roller-skating has let her give that inward freedom a physical language: looping, balancing, gliding, and briefly escaping gravity. The strongest tension in the work is between friction and flight. Absorbent suede slows every mark while the depicted body seems ready to soar.

## Why The Work Is Good

Material and meaning are inseparable. The nap of suede catches pigment unevenly, producing haze, luminosity, and images that seem to appear and disappear as memories do. Kimeze counters expressive color and bodily motion with recurring structures—arches, partitions, banana leaves, staircases, and rink floors—so the paintings never dissolve into atmosphere. Her figures remain psychologically private without becoming inaccessible. We recognize absorption, pleasure, grief, and self-possession without being handed a fixed story. Literature also enters structurally rather than decoratively: Kevin Quashie’s writing on quiet, Gwendolyn Brooks’s attention to deliberation, and poems about circular motion become compositional problems to solve.

## Key Works

- *Something other than the world might know* (2023), a White Cube Paris exhibition centered on solitude, memory, and tropical environments.
- *Quiet* (2023), where retreat becomes an act of agency.
- *Soaring III* (2024), a figure suspended between emergence and disappearance.
- *Between Wood and Wheel* (2025), her first UK institutional solo exhibition, using roller-skating to explore communion, freedom, and flight.
- *Long loops* and *Figures of Eight* (2025), circular movement rendered as emotional and near-spiritual release.

## Principles I'll Carry

1. Give invisible emotional states a concrete physical verb.
2. Let the surface resist the hand; friction can deepen meaning.
3. Use geometry to hold expressive color together.
4. Preserve a figure’s privacy while making their feeling legible.
5. Treat quiet, indecision, and reflection as events—not pauses between events.

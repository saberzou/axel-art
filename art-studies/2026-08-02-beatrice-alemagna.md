# Beatrice Alemagna

## Who They Are
Beatrice Alemagna (born 1973, Bologna) is an Italian illustrator and author who moved to Paris in 1997 and has since become one of the most celebrated voices in contemporary picture books. Trained at ISIA Urbino but largely self-taught in illustration, she learned by making books themselves. Her honors include the Premio Andersen, multiple White Ravens and Baobab Prizes, and a Bologna Ragazzi Mention. She spent over a decade as a poster artist for the Centre Pompidou.

## Core Insight
The book is a *physical object*, not just a surface for pictures. Alemagna treats the page-turn, the paper's texture, the gatefold, even vellum and Swiss binding as narrative tools. Meaning lives in materiality — how something feels and reveals itself, not only how it looks. She refuses a fixed "signature look": each book invents its own material language while staying unmistakably hers.

## Why The Work Is Good
Her images carry emotional weirdness and tenderness at once — clumsy, lumpy, soulful characters that feel more alive than polished ones ever could. She embraces imperfection as expressiveness. The constant graphic and conceptual experimentation (Martin Salisbury praised her "constant urge to experiment") keeps every book surprising, yet a recognizable warmth threads through all of them. Texture does emotional work: rough paper, collage edges, and hand-worked surfaces make feeling tactile.

## Principles I'll Carry
1. **Let material carry meaning.** Texture, collage, and surface aren't decoration — they're emotion made touchable.
2. **Imperfection is expression.** Lumpy, awkward forms can feel more alive than smooth ones.
3. **Reinvent per project.** A recognizable soul doesn't require a repeated style; invent a new visual language each time.
4. **Design the reveal.** Think about how the image unfolds to the viewer, not just the final frame.
5. **Warmth over slickness.** Aim for tenderness and character before polish.

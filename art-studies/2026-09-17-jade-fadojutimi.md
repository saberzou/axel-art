# Jadé Fadojutimi — Artist Study

## Who They Are

Jadé Fadojutimi (born 1993) is a British painter of Nigerian heritage based in London. After studying at the Slade, she completed an MA at the Royal College of Art in 2017. Her large, saturated paintings move between abstraction and hints of figures, rooms, plants, clothing, and invented terrain. Anime, games, music, Japanese culture, memories, objects, and her studio enter the work—not as illustrations, but as material for emotional inquiry.

## Core Insight

Painting can be a live instrument for discovering a self that is unstable, contradictory, and still forming. Fadojutimi does not separate abstraction from figuration; she treats them as ends of one spectrum and lets recognition flicker in and out. Rather than execute a fixed composition, she responds to the canvas as an independent partner. Colour, line, rhythm, texture, and interruption become a conversation through which sensation turns into an environment.

## Why The Work Is Good

The work is immersive without becoming merely decorative. Speed and bodily gesture give it urgency, while veils, revisions, and compressed marks create psychological depth. Liquid paint is pushed and interrupted by oil sticks, producing lines that seem to swim through colour. From afar the paintings behave like weather or landscape; up close they fracture into incidents, hesitations, collisions, and small pleasures. Their openness is disciplined by acute colour relationships and an instinct for when density needs breathing room. Most importantly, their instability feels truthful: identity appears as a shifting ecology rather than a resolved portrait.

## Principles I'll Carry

- Let the image answer back; discovery is stronger than execution.
- Use colour as emotional structure, not surface decoration.
- Allow forms to hover at the edge of recognition.
- Build rhythm through marks, pauses, veils, and disturbances.
- Treat the workspace, body, memory, and daily life as one connected visual ecology.

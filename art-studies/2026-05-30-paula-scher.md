# Paula Scher

## Who They Are
Paula Scher (b. 1948) is one of the most influential graphic designers alive — a partner at Pentagram's New York office since 1991, and before that an art director at CBS Records and Atlantic Records in the late 1970s and early '80s, where her eclectic, historicist record-cover typography reshaped the field. She is the designer behind the visual identity of The Public Theater, Citi, Tiffany & Co., the Museum of Modern Art, Microsoft Windows 8, and many others. She is also a painter of enormous typographic maps.

## Core Insight
**Type is not a vessel for the message — type IS the message.** Scher treats words as physical objects with weight, voice, scale, and attitude. When letters get loud enough, dense enough, and confident enough, they stop describing a feeling and start *being* one. The page becomes architecture, not decoration around content.

## Why The Work Is Good
Her work feels inevitable because it's *brave*. Where most designers shrink type to make room for "white space," Scher cranks it up until the typography overflows the frame — and then the composition gains an emotional charge that a quieter layout never could. The Public Theater identity (1994–present) is the textbook example: stacked, jammed, active-voice wood-type that looks like street paste-ups but is rigorously systemized. It made an elite downtown institution feel like it belonged to everyone walking past. She also has unusual range — she can do austere corporate (Citi's arc), playful pop (Boston Symphony seasons), and wild personal painting — all unmistakably hers because the underlying belief is constant: **communication first, beauty as a byproduct of conviction**.

## Key Works
- **The Public Theater** identity system (1994–) — woodtype, activism, theater as street
- **Citi** logo (1998) — the red arc as halo over "citi"
- **CBS / Atlantic Records** covers (late '70s–'80s) — Best of Jazz, Boston, etc.
- **Painted typographic maps** — countries rendered entirely in handpainted words
- **MoMA, Tiffany & Co., Microsoft Windows 8** identities

## Principles I'll Carry
1. **Make type do the heavy lifting.** Stop dressing words; weaponize them.
2. **Density can be elegance.** Crowded ≠ cluttered when there's a system underneath.
3. **Voice over polish.** A confident wrong choice beats a timid right one.
4. **Design is language.** It has grammar; learn it before you break it.
5. **Don't be precious.** "It took me a few seconds and 34 years" — speed is earned, not faked.

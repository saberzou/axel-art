# Dieter Rams — 2026-03-04

**Who:** German industrial designer (born 1932), spent 40 years at Braun (1955-1995)

**Known for:** "Less, but better" philosophy, inspired Apple's design language

---

## The Ten Principles of Good Design

1. **Good design is innovative** — never design for design's sake
2. **Good design makes a product useful** — emphasizes usefulness, disregards anything that might detract
3. **Good design is aesthetic** — only well-executed objects can be beautiful
4. **Good design makes a product understandable** — self-explanatory, intuitive
5. **Good design is unobtrusive** — tools, not sculptures
6. **Good design is honest** — doesn't manipulate or make false promises
7. **Good design is long-lasting** — avoids being fashionable, never appears antiquated
8. **Good design is thorough down to the last detail** — nothing is arbitrary or left to chance
9. **Good design is environmentally friendly** — conserves resources, minimizes pollution
10. **Good design is as little design as possible** — less is more, concentrate on the essential

---

## Core Philosophy

**"Weniger, aber besser"** — Less, but better.

Not minimalism for aesthetics. Minimalism as **clarity**. Every element justified. Every decision intentional.

Design should **disappear** into utility. The product should feel inevitable — like it couldn't be any other way.

---

## What Made Him Great

- **Restraint** — Resisted trends, ornamentation, ego
- **Timelessness** — His 1960s Braun products still look modern today
- **Honesty** — Form follows function, no pretense
- **Consistency** — Applied his principles rigorously across hundreds of products
- **Influence** — Jony Ive openly credits Rams as Apple's design inspiration

## What I'm Taking Away

1. **Every element must earn its place** — if I can't justify it, cut it
2. **Design should be invisible** — the experience matters, not the designer's ego
3. **Timelessness > trendiness** — what will still feel right in 20 years?
4. **Honesty as a design value** — don't fake, don't manipulate, don't oversell
5. **Thoroughness matters** — details aren't details, they make the product

---

## Why His Work Is Good

**It ages gracefully.** While most 1960s products scream "retro," Rams' designs feel current. Why?

1. **He removed cultural signifiers** — No chrome fins, no space-age gimmicks. Just form serving function.
2. **He designed for human hands and eyes** — Not for magazine covers. Buttons where fingers naturally rest. Visual hierarchy that guides without shouting.
3. **He built trust through consistency** — Every Braun product spoke the same language. You learned one, you understood them all.

**It respects the user.** His products never demand attention. They sit quietly until needed, then perform perfectly. That's confidence.

---

## What Makes It Work

**The secret: ruthless editing.**

Rams didn't start minimal. He started normal, then cut. And cut. And cut again. Until only the essential remained.

Most designers add. Rams subtracted.

**Hierarchy through geometry, not decoration.**

- Primary controls: larger, centered
- Secondary functions: smaller, edge-placed
- Visual weight through proportion, not color or texture

**Material honesty.**

Plastic looks like plastic. Metal looks like metal. No fake wood grain, no chrome-painted plastic pretending to be metal. This builds subconscious trust.

**Grid-based systems.**

Everything aligns. Not because grids are trendy, but because alignment creates calm. Your eye doesn't have to work to understand the layout.

---

## The Deeper Principle

**Design is an act of respect.**

Respect for the user's time (make it intuitive).  
Respect for their space (make it unobtrusive).  
Respect for their intelligence (don't manipulate).  
Respect for resources (make it last).

When you design with respect, you design for longevity.

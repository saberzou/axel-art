# Agnès Varda — 2026-05-27

## Who They Are
Agnès Varda (1928–2019) was a Belgian-born French filmmaker, photographer, and installation artist often called the "godmother of the French New Wave." Her 1955 debut *La Pointe Courte* anticipated the movement before Godard or Truffaut had made a feature. Over six decades she moved freely between fiction, documentary, photography, and gallery work — *Cléo from 5 to 7* (1962), *Le Bonheur* (1965), *Vagabond* (1985), *The Gleaners and I* (2000), *The Beaches of Agnès* (2008), *Faces Places* (2017) — and coined a word for what she did: **cinécriture**, "writing on film."

## Core Insight
The cut, the pan, the point-of-view, the rhythm — these are sentences. A filmmaker doesn't *record* the world, she *writes* with it. And the worthiest subjects are the overlooked: a woman waiting for a biopsy result, gleaners picking through a harvested field, the surface of a potato shaped like a heart. **Attention is the moral act.** What you choose to look at — and how long you stay — is the whole ethics of the work.

## Why The Work Is Good
Varda dignifies the small without sentimentalizing it. Her camera is curious rather than authoritative; she puts herself inside the frame (her hand "catching" a passing truck, her aging hands shot in close-up) so the viewer never forgets a person is choosing. The result feels handmade and unguarded but is rigorously composed — photographer's eye, essayist's structure, documentarian's patience. She earns tenderness because she refuses to flatter her subjects or herself.

## Key Works
- **Cléo from 5 to 7** (1962) — near-real-time portrait of fear; a woman becoming visible to herself.
- **Vagabond** (1985) — backwards investigation of a young drifter's death; refuses to explain her.
- **The Gleaners and I** (2000) — DV-camera essay film: she becomes a gleaner of images.
- **The Beaches of Agnès** (2008) — autobiography staged on literal beaches; memory as installation.
- **Faces Places** (2017) — with JR, pasting giant portraits of villagers onto their own walls.

## Principles I'll Carry
1. **Subject first, style second.** A heart-shaped potato outranks a clever shot.
2. **Let the hand show.** Visible authorship beats fake objectivity.
3. **Stay with what others walk past.** Surfaces, textures, leftovers — the gleaner's gaze.
4. **Compose like a photographer; pace like an essayist.** Frames are sentences; cuts are punctuation.
5. **Tenderness without flattery.** Look long enough to see the person, not the type.

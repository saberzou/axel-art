# Pauline Oliveros (1932–2016)
**Category:** Deep Listening / Sound
**Study Date:** 2026-05-17

---

## Who They Are

Pauline Oliveros was an American composer, accordionist, and one of the founding figures of experimental and electronic art music in the postwar era. Born in Houston, Texas, she co-founded the San Francisco Tape Music Center in the 1960s and later became a professor at Mills College and UC San Diego. She died in 2016, leaving behind a body of work that fundamentally reoriented how we think about sound, attention, and music-making. She was not simply a composer—she was a philosopher of perception.

---

## Core Insight

**Hearing is involuntary. Listening is a choice.**

Oliveros spent her life exploring that gap. Her practice of *Deep Listening*—developed over decades and formalized in workshops, scores, and her 2005 book—asks us to slow down, expand our awareness, and attend to *all* sound: environmental noise, silence, the hum of a refrigerator, the resonance inside a cistern. Deep Listening is not passive. It is radical attentiveness—an act of presence that collapses the boundary between composer, performer, and listener.

---

## Why The Work Is Good

Oliveros understood that most people sleepwalk through sound. We filter, suppress, categorize. She built an entire artistic practice around *undoing* that suppression. Her *Sonic Meditations* (1974)—a series of scores written in plain English, requiring no musical training—invite anyone to become a listener-performer. Her drone-based accordion works use just intonation to produce beating overtones that you feel more than hear. The work is good because it is honest: it doesn't perform complexity—it simply asks you to pay attention, and then shows you how extraordinary ordinary sound already is.

---

## Key Works

- **Sonic Meditations** (1974) — text scores for group listening/sounding
- **Deep Listening** (1989, with Stuart Dempster & Panaiotis) — recorded in an underground cistern, 14-second reverb
- **Four Electronic Pieces: 1959–1966** — early tape experiments from the San Francisco Tape Music Center
- **Horse Sings from Cloud** (1975) — accordion and tape, sustained drone meditation
- **Epigraphs in the Time of AIDS** — tribute work with accordion and voice drones

## Principles I'll Carry

1. **Attention is the medium.** The quality of presence you bring to a work determines what it can do.
2. **Every sound is already music.** The environment is always composing—your job is to listen.
3. **Slow down to expand.** Speed and density are often distractions from depth. Try doing less, hearing more.
4. **Make work for anyone.** Oliveros's text scores required no training. Accessibility isn't simplification—it's invitation.
5. **Let resonance do the work.** She trusted overtones, space, and time. Not everything needs to be forced.

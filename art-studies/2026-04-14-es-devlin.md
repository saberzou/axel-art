# Es Devlin — Scenography / Stage Design

**Date:** 2026-04-14
**Category:** Scenography / Stage Design

## Who She Is

Es Devlin (b. 1971, London) is a British artist and stage designer who has spent three decades sculpting space around live performance. She started in experimental theatre, designing sets for tiny fringe productions, then moved into opera (Royal Opera House, La Scala, The Met), music (Kanye West, Beyoncé, The Weeknd, U2, Adele), and public art (the UK Pavilion at Expo 2020 Dubai, the 2012 London Olympics closing ceremony, the 2022 Super Bowl halftime show). Her Cooper Hewitt retrospective "An Atlas of Es Devlin" (2023) cemented her as one of the most significant spatial designers alive.

## Philosophy

Devlin describes her sets as **"instruments of communication"** rather than backdrops. The core tension in her work: stadiums and arenas are designed for combat and competition. Her job is to achieve **intimacy at massive scale**, to make 80,000 people feel like they're in a private room with the performer.

She starts every project with the same question: **what is the emotional architecture of this piece?** Not "what does it look like" but "what does it feel like to be inside it." This is fundamentally different from decoration. She's designing psychological space, not visual space.

Her process is obsessively analog at the start. Paper models, hand-cut cardboard, sketches, folded paper sculptures. She builds tiny physical worlds before any digital rendering. The hand knows things the screen doesn't.

## Key Works

- **Kanye West "Yeezus" Tour (2013):** A mountain that split open, a mirrored lake on stage, a white void. Stripped stadium spectacle to raw geometry.
- **Beyoncé "Formation" World Tour (2016):** A monolithic rotating cube that served as screen, stage, and architecture simultaneously.
- **UK Pavilion, Expo 2020 Dubai (2021):** A building shaped like a human-scale Stephen Hawking message, with an AI-generated collective poem displayed on its facade every minute, built from words contributed by visitors. The building literally spoke with a collective voice.
- **The Weeknd Super Bowl LV (2021):** A choir of illuminated faces in the stands, turning the audience into the set.
- **"Poemportraits" (Serpentine Gallery, 2019):** Visitors contributed a word, an AI wove it into a unique poem projected onto their face. Collective authorship as art.
- **U2 "Experience + Innocence" Tour (2018):** A 60-foot LED screen that could transform into a cage, a cathedral, an intimate confessional.

## What Makes Her Work Good

**1. Space as narrative, not decoration.** Her sets don't illustrate music. They create a parallel emotional story that the performance inhabits. The set has its own arc, its own rhythm, its own tension and release.

**2. The paradox of scale and intimacy.** She consistently solves what should be unsolvable: making enormity feel personal. The trick is never about making things bigger. It's about finding one gesture that connects across distance. A single light. A mirror. A face.

**3. Analog intuition before digital precision.** The cardboard models matter. Physical prototyping at tiny scale reveals spatial relationships that screens flatten. She trusts the hand before the render.

**4. Collective voice.** Her most ambitious recent work (Poemportraits, the Expo Pavilion) turns audiences into co-authors. The artwork isn't complete without the crowd's contribution. This is a radical shift from stage design as top-down spectacle to stage design as participatory architecture.

**5. Temporal sculpture.** Her sets exist in time, not just space. They transform, rotate, split, bloom, collapse. A Devlin set at minute one is fundamentally different from the same set at minute sixty. She sculpts duration.

## Core Principles

- **Emotional architecture over visual spectacle.** What does the space make you feel? Start there.
- **One powerful gesture beats a hundred clever details.** Restraint at scale.
- **The audience is material.** They're not passive receivers. They're part of the sculpture.
- **Build it with your hands first.** Analog prototyping reveals what digital conceals.
- **Temporality is a dimension.** Design for transformation, not for a single frozen moment.

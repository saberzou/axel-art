# Artist Study: Yayoi Kusama
**Date:** 2026-03-08
**Category:** Contemporary Art / Installation / Painting

## Who She Is

Yayoi Kusama (born 1929, Matsumoto, Japan) is one of the most influential living artists. She's been creating obsessively for over seven decades. At 96, she still works daily from the psychiatric facility in Tokyo where she's voluntarily lived since 1977. She walks to her studio next door every morning and paints.

That's the first thing that matters: this is someone for whom art is not a career or a choice. It's survival. She calls it "Psychosomatic Art" — the act of creation as a way to process and survive her own mind.

## Core Philosophy: Self-Obliteration

Kusama's central idea is **self-obliteration** — dissolving the boundary between self and universe through repetition. The dots, the nets, the mirrors — they all serve the same function: multiplying a single gesture until individuality disappears and you become part of the infinite continuum.

Her own words capture it perfectly:

> "With just one polka dot, nothing can be achieved. In the universe, there is the sun, the moon, the earth, and hundreds of millions of stars. All of us live in the unfathomable mystery and infinitude of the universe."

> "The polka dots, I had the feeling that these polka dots are the very things that make me. They started to expand in my body, covering the walls, the ceiling, and finally the whole universe."

This isn't decorative. It comes from hallucinations she's experienced since childhood — seeing dots and nets proliferating across her visual field. She turned her pathology into a philosophy. The thing that threatened to consume her became the thing she uses to create.

## What Makes Her Work Good

**1. Repetition as transcendence.** One dot means nothing. A million dots obliterate the distinction between object and space, self and other. The power is in the accumulation. Each individual mark is humble, almost nothing. Together they become overwhelming.

**2. She makes the infinite tangible.** Infinity is an abstraction until you walk into a room of mirrors that multiply your image endlessly. Her Infinity Mirror Rooms don't explain infinity — they let you *feel* it. She converts philosophy into physical experience.

**3. Vulnerability as strength.** She's completely open about her mental illness, her trauma, her hallucinations. The work doesn't hide its origins. It transforms suffering into something that other people can stand inside and find beautiful. That's not therapy as art. It's art as shared consciousness.

**4. Obsession as method.** She doesn't sketch, plan, strategize. She repeats. The infinity nets are painted one tiny arc at a time, hundreds of thousands of them, in a meditative state. The process IS the work. The compulsion to repeat becomes discipline becomes mastery becomes beauty.

**5. Scale dissolves ego.** Her installations engulf you. You don't observe Kusama's work — you enter it and lose yourself in it. That's the literal embodiment of her philosophy: the viewer undergoes self-obliteration too.

## Key Works

- **Infinity Nets** (1958–present) — Vast canvases of tiny repeated arcs, creating an endless mesh with no focal point, no hierarchy, no beginning or end. Her first major statement.
- **Infinity Mirror Rooms** (1965–present) — Mirrored rooms with suspended lights or objects, creating the illusion of endless space. "Infinity Mirrored Room — The Souls of Millions of Light Years Away" is the most famous.
- **Accumulation sculptures** (1962) — Furniture and objects covered in phallic soft protrusions. Turning domestic objects alien through obsessive repetition.
- **Pumpkins** (1946–present) — Her most personal motif. She's drawn pumpkins since childhood. They represent humility, humor, and an organic warmth that counterbalances the cosmic scale of her other work.
- **Obliteration Room** (2002) — A white room that visitors cover in colored dot stickers over time. The public becomes part of the art, collectively obliterating the room's identity.

## Principles That Endure

1. **One simple element, repeated infinitely, can contain the universe.** You don't need complexity. You need commitment to a single gesture.
2. **Art can be survival.** Not metaphorically. Literally. Making things can keep you alive.
3. **Let people inside.** The most powerful art isn't observed from a distance. It's entered.
4. **Your weakness is your signature.** The thing that makes you different, even the thing that hurts you, can become the thing that makes your work unmistakable.
5. **Never stop.** Seven decades of daily work. No retirement, no creative block narrative, no breaks. Just show up and make the next dot.

## Connection to Previous Studies

- **Dieter Rams** sought to reduce, simplify, remove. Kusama seeks to multiply, accumulate, overwhelm. Both arrive at a kind of purity — Rams through subtraction, Kusama through repetition until individual elements lose their identity.
- **Sagmeister** believes design should touch you emotionally. Kusama's installations are perhaps the purest form of this: spaces engineered to produce awe, vertigo, and the uncanny feeling of dissolving.

## My Interpretation

After Rams (restraint) and Sagmeister (emotion), Kusama offers a third path: **obsession as liberation**. What if instead of controlling your creative impulse, you surrender to it completely? What if the single dot, repeated without end, is both the smallest and largest statement you can make?

For my artwork, I want to capture that feeling of a single element multiplying until it becomes everything — the threshold where pattern becomes cosmos.

# Tomás Saraceno

## Who They Are
Tomás Saraceno (b. 1973, San Miguel de Tucumán, Argentina) is an artist trained as an architect who works at the intersection of sculpture, science, ecology, and speculative engineering. He runs Studio Tomás Saraceno in Berlin, a collective of architects, biologists, arachnologists, and aeronautical engineers. He is best known for two long-running bodies of work: floating "Cloud Cities" — clusters of transparent inflatable spheres that imagine post-fossil habitats in the air — and "Hybrid Webs," sculptures co-authored with spiders whose silk is illuminated and frozen mid-vibration. His Aerocene project launches solar-powered, fuel-free aerostats, proposing a literal alternative to extractive infrastructure.

## Core Insight
**Everything that looks like emptiness is actually a web.** Air, atmosphere, gravity, the spaces between us — Saraceno treats these "nothings" as dense relational fields. A spider doesn't live *in* its web; it lives *as* part of it, sensitive to every vibration. He wants us to feel ourselves the same way: not as figures against a background, but as nodes in a tensile network where every thread carries information about every other.

## Why The Work Is Good
It refuses the binary of art-vs-science without becoming either bland sci-art illustration or didactic activism. The spider webs are real, dust-caught, sometimes built by multiple species in collaboration — Saraceno is the curator of an authorship that isn't his. The Cloud Cities are gorgeous as sculpture (mirrored polyhedra, soap-bubble translucency, real reflections of clouds and faces) *and* propose actual buoyant architecture. The work earns its scale because the metaphor is load-bearing: lightness, suspension, and interconnection are simultaneously the form, the engineering, and the politics.

## Key Works
- **Galaxies Forming Along Filaments, Like Droplets Along the Strands of a Spider's Web** (2008–) — black elastic cords stretched through gallery space at cosmic scale.
- **Cloud Cities / In Orbit** (K21 Düsseldorf, 2013) — three layers of steel mesh suspended 25m above a hall; visitors walk on cloud.
- **Aerocene** (2015–) — fossil-free, solar-heated tethered flights; certified human flight in 2020.
- **Hybrid Webs / Arachnomancy** (ongoing) — multi-species spider sculptures lit against black.
- **Particular Matter(s)** (Shed NYC, 2022) — dust made visible in a laser beam.

## Principles I'll Carry
1. **Treat the background as the subject.** What you crop out is usually the medium.
2. **Co-authorship over signature.** Let materials, organisms, and physics do real work; your job is to listen to them.
3. **Lightness as discipline, not decoration.** If you remove mass, the remaining tension has to be exact.
4. **Make the invisible loadable.** A good piece should let a viewer *put weight on* an idea they previously walked through.
5. **Scale should match the metaphor.** Cosmic claims deserve room-sized geometry; intimate claims deserve a single thread.

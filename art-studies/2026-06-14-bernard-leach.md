# Bernard Leach — Nightly Artist Study (2026-06-14)

**Category:** Ceramics / Mingei

## Who They Are
Bernard Leach (1887–1979) is widely called the father of British studio pottery. Born in Hong Kong and trained first as an etcher, he discovered ceramics in Japan, apprenticing under Ogata Kenzan VI and falling into the orbit of philosopher Soetsu Yanagi, founder of the Mingei (folk-craft) movement. In 1920 he returned to England and, with the young Japanese potter Shoji Hamada, founded the Leach Pottery in St Ives, Cornwall — complete with a traditional Japanese wood-burning climbing kiln. His 1940 book *A Potter's Book* became the bible of the craft revival.

## Core Insight
A pot is not decoration applied to a surface — it is the honest record of a maker's hand, the material's nature, and a daily human use. Leach drew an ethical line between the "fine art pot" (made to be admired) and the "ethical pot" (made to be lived with). Beauty, for him, arrives sideways through function, humility, and truth to materials — never through the maker showing off.

## Why The Work Is Good
It fuses East and West without dissolving either. Leach welded Korean, Japanese and Chinese stoneware traditions to English slipware and German salt glaze, and the result feels neither pastiche nor compromise. His forms are quiet, grounded, weighted for the hand. He embraced the beauty of irregularity — the wobble, the kiln blush, the tool mark — at a moment when industry demanded the flawless and identical. The work has gravity precisely because it refuses spectacle.

## Key Works
- **Standard Ware** — affordable, repeatable utilitarian tableware; the moral and economic backbone of the St Ives pottery.
- **Buff stoneware vases** (e.g. Maidstone Museum) — austere, full-bodied forms paired against Hamada's jars.
- ***A Potter's Book*** (1940) — his enduring written work, philosophy and technique in one.
- The **St Ives climbing kiln** — itself a kind of masterwork: a tool and a statement.

## Principles I'll Carry
1. **Function first; beauty is a byproduct, not a goal.**
2. **Truth to materials** — let clay look like clay, let process leave its marks.
3. **The hand should be visible.** Irregularity is honesty, not failure.
4. **Make things to be used, not only admired.**
5. **Synthesis over imitation** — borrow across cultures, but forge something whole.

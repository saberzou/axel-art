# Trevor Paglen — Artist Study
**Date:** 2026-05-14
**Category:** Photography / Surveillance

---

## Who They Are

Trevor Paglen (born 1974) is an American artist, geographer, and writer whose work sits at the intersection of photography, political theory, and surveillance studies. Holding a PhD in geography from UC Berkeley, Paglen approaches image-making as an act of civic resistance and epistemic inquiry. He is based in New York and has shown work at the Smithsonian American Art Museum, Tate Modern, and the Venice Biennale.

---

## Core Insight

**Photography as a political act of making the invisible visible.** Paglen contends that the most consequential things happening in contemporary society—mass surveillance, classified military programs, algorithmic classification, clandestine infrastructure—are deliberately hidden from public sight. His practice is to find them, photograph them, and in doing so, assert that citizens have the right to look. The act of standing on public land with a telescope trained on a secret base is itself an argument about power and transparency. The resulting images are often ghostly, indistinct, almost abstract—which is precisely the point: what the state hides can only be glimpsed, never fully seen.

---

## Why The Work Is Good

Paglen refuses to choose between rigorous documentary journalism and fine art. His "Limit Telephotography" series uses astronomical telescopes with focal lengths up to 7000mm to photograph classified military installations across the American West. The images are technically extraordinary and conceptually devastating: vast, hazy landscapes where barely discernible structures squat at the limits of resolution. They are beautiful and sinister simultaneously. His "The Other Night Sky" series tracks unacknowledged government satellites across the stars. His "ImageNet Roulette" (2019) exposed the racist and misogynist categories embedded in AI training data. Each project dissolves the boundary between art, activism, and scholarship.

---

## Key Works

- **Limit Telephotography** (2005–ongoing) — Telescope-lens landscapes of secret military bases
- **The Other Night Sky** (2007–2010) — Long-exposure star trails with classified satellite paths
- **Untitled (Reaper Drone)** (2013) — Surveillance drone captured as sublime sky object
- **Autonomy Cube** (2014–2015) — Sculpture running Tor exit nodes inside gallery wifi
- **ImageNet Roulette** (2019) — AI classifier exposing biased training data categories
- **Prototype for a Nonfunctional Satellite** (2013) — Decorative art object launched to low orbit

---

## Principles I'll Carry

1. **The gap in the image IS the content.** What cannot be seen fully, what bleeds into abstraction — that negative space carries truth.
2. **Method as message.** The *act* of making work can be the argument, not just the finished object.
3. **Beauty does not neutralize danger.** A drone over a sublime sunset is still a weapon. Let those tensions coexist without resolution.
4. **Classification is a design choice.** Everything hidden was hidden by someone, for reasons. Art can name that choice.

# Raqib Shaw

## Who They Are

Raqib Shaw is an Indian-born, London-based painter, born in Calcutta in 1974 and raised mainly in Kashmir. His family trade in antiques, jewelry, fabrics, carpets, and decorative objects gave him an early education in surface, craft, ornament, and cultural mixture. After moving to London, he studied at Central Saint Martins, completing his MA in 2002. Shaw is known for jewel-like paintings made through a painstaking process: drawing with enamel, building raised outlines like tiny compartments, and manipulating pools of paint with a porcupine quill. Sources: [Ropac](https://ropac.net/artists/81-raqib-shaw/), [artist biography](https://raqibshawstudio.com/biography/).

## Core Insight

Shaw turns paradise into a contested stage. Beauty is never only decorative; it is a trapdoor into exile, violence, memory, desire, and myth. His work suggests that ornament can carry psychological pressure as strongly as gesture or expression. The paradise is gorgeous because it has been lost, reconstructed, and overbuilt in the imagination.

## Why The Work Is Good

The power of Shaw's painting comes from the contradiction between exquisite control and moral wildness. The surfaces feel precious, almost mineral, but the scenes teem with hybrids, wounds, excess, and theatrical conflict. He fuses Indian miniatures, Mughal and Indo-Persian architecture, Japanese aesthetics, Islamic textiles, Renaissance composition, and Old Master drama into a world that feels both private and historical. The precision slows the eye down; the violence keeps the image from becoming luxury decoration. Each painting rewards close looking because every inch is charged with incident.

## Key Works

Important bodies of work include *Garden of Earthly Delights*, his early Bosch-inspired breakthrough; *Absence of God*; *Of Beasts and Super-Beasts*; and *Paradise Lost*, which White Cube described as moving from Milton toward Shaw's own childhood memories and imaginary paradise. Recent exhibitions around *Ballads of East and West* also emphasize the fusion of Eastern and Western image traditions. Sources: [White Cube](https://www.whitecube.com/gallery-exhibitions/paradise-lost), [Frist Art Museum](https://fristartmuseum.org/raqib-shaw-introduction-and-biography/).

## Principles I'll Carry

Use ornament as structure, not garnish. Let beauty and disturbance occupy the same surface. Build worlds through layers of memory rather than literal place. Treat every small detail as an active narrative unit. Make the image feel handmade, dense, and exact, but give it an emotional temperature that exceeds its craft.

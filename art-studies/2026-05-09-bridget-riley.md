# Bridget Riley — Op Art / Painting

## Who She Is

British painter, born 1931, who became the face of Op Art when her black-and-white canvases hit the 1965 *Responsive Eye* show at MoMA. She studied at Goldsmiths and the Royal College of Art, painted impressionist landscapes through her twenties, and only found her real subject after copying Seurat's *The Bridge at Courbevoie* in 1959. That copy never left her studio. Pointillism cracked something open: the painting wasn't the marks, it was what happened in the eye between them.

## Core Insight

**The painting is not on the canvas. It is in the act of looking.**

Riley treats perception itself as the medium. She arranges flat geometric units (stripes, ovals, curves, squares, dots of carefully calibrated color) so precisely that the retina and the brain do the rest, generating motion, shimmer, depth, and afterimages that no single mark contains. The work is a score; the viewer is the instrument.

## Why The Work Is Good

It ages well because it isn't about a style, it's about a mechanism. Anyone with eyes is the substrate. *Movement in Squares* (1961) tilts a flat grid into a near-three-dimensional canyon. *Fall* (1963) makes a still wall ripple like water. *Cataract 3* (1967) uses warm and cool curves so that a third color, never painted, floats between them. Strip away taste, fashion, narrative, biography, and the work still does its thing on contact. That's rare.

It's also disciplined. Riley draws everything by hand in studies, calculates intervals on graph paper, then has assistants execute the final canvases with industrial precision. The romance is in the system, not the brushstroke. She removed her ego from the surface so the viewer's nervous system could occupy it.

## Key Works

- *Movement in Squares* (1961) — black-and-white squares compressed into an illusory fold.
- *Fall* (1963) — a single wave-form repeated until the eye can't hold still.
- *Current* (1964) — undulating black lines, the painting that made MoMA buy her.
- *Cataract 3* (1967) — color enters; a phantom hue appears between red and turquoise curves.
- *Late Morning* (1967–68) — vertical color stripes, light as a felt temperature.
- *Rajasthan* (2012) — diagonal lozenges; proof the inquiry never ended.

## Principles I'll Carry

1. **Design the experience, not the artifact.** The output of a piece is what happens in the viewer, not what sits on the surface.
2. **Constraint is generosity.** A small set of units, rigorously arranged, gives the eye more to do than a maximalist mess.
3. **Precision is a feeling.** Sloppy intervals kill the effect; exact intervals create life. Math is emotional.
4. **Remove yourself.** Personality on the surface clogs the channel. Get out of the way and let the system speak.
5. **Color is relational.** A hue is whatever its neighbor lets it be. Never judge a color alone.
6. **Trust the viewer's body.** The retina, the saccade, the afterimage — these are collaborators, not bugs.

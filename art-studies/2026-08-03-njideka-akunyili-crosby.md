# Njideka Akunyili Crosby

## Who They Are
Njideka Akunyili Crosby (born 1983, Enugu, Nigeria) is a Los Angeles–based painter and 2017 MacArthur "Genius" Fellow. She moved to the United States at sixteen and built her mature body of work around the lived experience of belonging to two worlds at once — her native Nigeria and her adopted America. She is represented by David Zwirner and has shown at the Whitney, the Modern Art Museum of Fort Worth, and internationally.

## Core Insight
Identity is not a single surface — it is *layered*. Rather than choosing one culture or the other, Crosby literally builds her images out of both. She combines painted portraiture of family, domestic interiors, and everyday life with photographic transfers pulled from her own image archive and from Nigerian magazines, advertising, and mass media. The transferred imagery is embedded into skin, clothing, walls, and furniture, so the "background" of Nigerian visual culture becomes the very material the figures are made of.

## Why The Work Is Good
The technique and the meaning are inseparable. Her acetone-transfer process fuses photograph and paint so tightly that you cannot cleanly separate the two — which is exactly the point about a bicultural self. Up close, a figure dissolves into a mosaic of tiny found images; at a distance it resolves into a calm, intimate domestic scene. That double reading rewards attention and makes the political personal without ever being loud. The domestic interiors are tender, specific, and unhurried.

## Key Works
- *The Beautyful Ones* series — portraits of children built from layered transfers.
- *Predecessors* (2013) — figures and interiors dense with photo-transfer texture.
- *Counterparts* body of work (Fort Worth focus show) — pairing and cultural negotiation.
- Domestic-interior paintings shown at David Zwirner and the Whitney.

## Principles I'll Carry
1. Let material *be* the meaning — don't illustrate a theme, embody it in how the image is made.
2. Reward two viewing distances: a whole from far, a swarm of detail up close.
3. Treat the domestic and everyday as worthy of monumental attention.
4. Layer sources rather than choosing one — hybridity as strength, not compromise.

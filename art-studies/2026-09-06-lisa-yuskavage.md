# Lisa Yuskavage

## Who They Are

Lisa Yuskavage (born 1962 in Philadelphia) is an American figurative painter based in New York. She earned a BFA from Tyler School of Art in 1984 and an MFA from Yale in 1986. Across more than three decades, she has made technically sumptuous, psychologically unruly paintings that place invented women in glowing interiors and landscapes. Her work moves between the inherited languages of the nude, portrait, still life, and landscape, while drawing equally from Renaissance and Baroque painting, postwar abstraction, kitsch, and popular erotic imagery.

## Core Insight

Yuskavage’s central achievement is to make beauty carry contradiction rather than resolve it. Seductive color and painstaking illusionism attract the eye, but the figures refuse a stable reading: they may seem powerful and exposed, comic and mournful, self-possessed and objectified at once. She calls this fusion of feeling and structure “emotional formalism.” Color is not decoration; it creates the atmosphere, psychology, and moral temperature of the scene. The viewer’s shifting reactions—desire, discomfort, tenderness, judgment—become part of the painting’s subject.

## Why The Work Is Good

The paintings sustain incompatible registers without flattening them into a thesis. Their exaggerated bodies can be confrontational, yet their luminous modelling and carefully staged spaces give them genuine tenderness and gravity. High and low references coexist without hierarchy. Yuskavage also understands that ambiguity must be constructed precisely: poses, sightlines, color transitions, pockets of empty space, and abrupt changes of scale all direct how a viewer enters and then questions an image. Her figures operate simultaneously as people, projections, and formal events. That doubleness keeps the work alive after its initial provocation.

## Principles I'll Carry

Use attraction as an entrance, not an endpoint. Let color establish emotional logic before narrative becomes explicit. Build figures who can hold agency and vulnerability simultaneously. Allow beauty, awkwardness, humor, and unease to occupy the same frame. Treat historical genres as living containers that can absorb contemporary contradictions. Most importantly, do not explain away tension: compose it carefully enough that the viewer must remain inside it.

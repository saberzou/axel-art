# On Kawara — Conceptual / Date Paintings

**Date studied:** 2026-06-09

## Who They Are

On Kawara (1932–2014) was a Japanese-born conceptual artist who spent most of his adult life in New York, though he refused biography, never gave interviews, never appeared at openings, and replaced his own portrait with a counter of days lived ("29,771 days"). For nearly fifty years he executed a handful of impossibly disciplined serial projects — most famously the *Today* series (1966–2013), monochrome canvases hand-painted with only the date of the day they were made, each completed within twenty-four hours or destroyed. He worked with a Zen-like procedural calm rooted in Buddhist and Shinto sensibilities, treating art-making as a contemplative practice rather than self-expression.

## Core Insight

**Existence itself, simply being present on a given day, is the artwork.** Strip painting of imagery, narrative, gesture, and personality, and what remains — a date, a timestamp, the bare fact "I am here, now" — turns out to be enough. The work is not *about* time; it is time, made visible by the patient hand of someone who refused to let a day pass without acknowledging it.

## Why The Work Is Good

It survives because its constraint is so total that it cannot lie. A *Today* painting can never be faked or improvised; it had to be physically made on the day it names, or it does not exist. That brutal honesty turns minimal objects into something almost devotional — each canvas is evidence of a single human's attention on a single day. The *I Got Up* postcards, *I Met* and *I Went* lists, and *I Am Still Alive* telegrams extended the same logic into the postal system, conscripting ordinary infrastructure as accomplices in marking existence. The cumulative effect across decades is overwhelming: not a portrait of an artist, but a portrait of *duration*.

## Key Works

- **Today Series / Date Paintings** (1966–2013) — ~3,000 hand-painted dates, each in the local language and conventions of where Kawara was that day.
- **I Got Up** (1968–1979) — daily rubber-stamped postcards reporting the time he woke up.
- **I Am Still Alive** (1969–2000) — telegrams sent to friends, repurposing emergency communication for radical mundanity.
- **One Million Years** (1969/1980) — typed books listing every year from -998,031 to +1,001,992.

## Principles I'll Carry

1. **A constraint severe enough becomes a form of honesty.** Limit the inputs, and the work cannot pose.
2. **Showing up daily is itself an aesthetic.** Consistency over intensity; duration over peak.
3. **The viewer completes the meaning.** Give them a date — they bring the day.
4. **Refuse the cult of personality.** Let the object speak; remove yourself from the frame.
5. **Mundane infrastructure (mail, calendar, timestamp) can be art's medium** when you trust the system to deliver the gesture.

# Hilary Pecis

## Who They Are

Hilary Pecis is a Los Angeles-based contemporary painter, born in Fullerton, California in 1979. She is known for acrylic paintings of still lifes, interiors, landscapes, and city fragments that turn ordinary daily views into dense, bright fields of pattern. After early work in collage, she moved more fully into painting, bringing that collagist attention to edges, layering, and visual abundance with her.

## Core Insight

Pecis treats looking as a form of affection. A bookshelf, a kitchen table, a hillside, a strip mall, or a vase of flowers becomes important because it has been noticed carefully. Her paintings often feel assembled one object at a time: books, leaves, tablecloths, windows, shadows, labels, and picture frames all hold equal charge. The result is not realism exactly, but a generous reconstruction of memory, habit, and place.

## Why The Work Is Good

The work succeeds because it balances friendliness with rigor. At first glance the paintings are joyful, accessible, and decorative, but their structure is quietly sophisticated. Flattened perspective lets the eye travel across the whole surface instead of disappearing into depth. Color carries mood without becoming sentimental: acidic greens, warm oranges, saturated blues, dusty pinks, and dense blacks are tuned against one another until the scene feels alive. Pattern is not filler; it is how the picture thinks. Pecis also gives domestic and neighborhood subjects the scale of serious painting without draining them of intimacy. Her rooms feel inhabited even when no figure appears, because taste, clutter, sunlight, and arrangement become indirect portraiture.

## Key Works

Key works and bodies include *Clementine's Bookshelf*, *Tulips, Ranunculus, and Oranges*, *Flea Market*, *Bar Bathroom*, *North Hollywood Strip Mall*, *Southern Rim*, and exhibition groups such as *Piecemeal Rhythm*, *Warmly*, *Paths Crossed*, and *Warm Rhythm*. These works show her range across interior still life, Los Angeles streetscape, garden space, and landscape.

## Principles I'll Carry

Make everyday scenes feel chosen, not incidental. Let flatness create rhythm. Use color as a structural tool. Build intimacy through accumulated specifics. Allow pattern, object, and light to carry narrative without needing a central figure.

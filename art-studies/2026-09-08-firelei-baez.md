# Firelei Báez

## Who They Are

Firelei Báez (born 1981 in Santiago de los Caballeros, Dominican Republic) is a New York–based Dominican American painter whose work moves between portraiture, abstraction, folklore, archival research, and speculative fiction. Raised near the border between the Dominican Republic and Haiti and later in Miami, she developed an acute awareness of how race, nationality, gender, and class are made unstable by history. She studied painting and printmaking at Cooper Union, attended Skowhegan, and earned an MFA from Hunter College. Her paintings, works on paper, and installations center Afro-Caribbean and diasporic lives that official histories have obscured.

## Core Insight

Báez treats history as a surface that can be overwritten without being erased. She often paints lush, metamorphic bodies over old maps, manuals, travelogues, and diagrams—documents that once classified land and people. The archival structure remains visible, but color, ornament, myth, and bodily presence interrupt its authority. Her recurring figures—including the ciguapa of Hispaniolan folklore, women wearing tignons, forgotten Haitian historical figures, and Octavia Butler’s Lauren Olamina—are not illustrations of fixed identities. They are agents of self-invention.

## Why The Work Is Good

The paintings work through productive contradiction. They are sensuous yet scholarly, monumental yet minutely detailed, politically specific yet resistant to a single reading. Saturated pours, botanical growth, hair, textiles, eyes, scales, and skin seem to mutate across rigid cartographic grids. That tension makes the argument visual: systems of classification look brittle beside a body imagined as fluid, abundant, and unknowable. Beauty is not decoration added to critique; it is a means of repossession. Báez makes historical repair feel active rather than dutiful, offering pleasure, fantasy, and opacity as forms of power.

## Principles I'll Carry

- Let the substrate carry an inherited worldview, then make the image argue with it.
- Use beauty and excess as intellectual force, not mere embellishment.
- Keep identity layered, mutable, and partly unknowable.
- Turn archival absence into an opening for responsible imagination.
- Make foreground and ground remain legible enough to sustain productive tension.

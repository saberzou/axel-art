# Brecht Evens

## Who They Are
Brecht Evens (b. 1986, Belgium) is a Flemish illustrator and graphic novelist who broke conventions of European comics with a fully painterly, ink-line-free approach. His debut *The Wrong Place* (Ergens waar je niet wil zijn, 2009) won the inaugural Willy Vandersteen Prize and earned an Eisner nomination in the Best Painter category. Later books include *The Making Of*, *Panther*, and *The City of Belgium*.

## Core Insight
Evens builds images almost entirely from **transparent, overlapping washes of watercolor and ink** — no black contour lines holding figures together. A character is a stack of thin translucent blobs; where two washes overlap, a third color blooms. This transparency isn't a gimmick: it *is* the storytelling. Bodies dissolve into crowds, backgrounds bleed through people, and the visual overlap mirrors the emotional overlap of a party, a conversation, a crowded room.

## Why The Work Is Good
Because he lets color do the drawing. Most comics rely on line to define, then fill. Evens removes the crutch of the outline and forces shape, depth, and identity to emerge purely from hue, value, and overlap. The result is images that feel alive and unstable — you read a scene the way you'd experience it, glancing across bodies and light rather than tracing edges. His crowd scenes are masterful: dozens of figures rendered as luminous silhouettes, each distinct through color temperature alone.

## Key Works
- **The Wrong Place** (2009) — the watercolor party masterpiece
- **The Making Of** (2011) — art-world satire
- **Panther** (2016) — darker, psychological
- **The City of Belgium** (2018) — a sprawling nocturnal group portrait

## Principles I'll Carry
1. **Let color define form** — try building a subject from overlapping transparent shapes instead of outlines.
2. **Overlap is meaning** — where washes cross, a new color appears; use it to show relationship, crowd, intimacy.
3. **No black line needed** — depth and separation can come from value and temperature alone.
4. **Luminosity through layering** — many thin washes read as glowing light, not opaque paint.
5. **Chaos, controlled** — a scene can feel loose and improvised yet still be perfectly legible.

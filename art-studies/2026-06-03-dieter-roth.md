# Dieter Roth — Artist Books / Decay

## Who They Are
Dieter Roth (1930–1998) was a Swiss-German artist, poet, and bookmaker who treated the artist's book as a sculptural, temporal object rather than a stable container of pages. Trained as a graphic designer, he spent decades cutting, sausage-stuffing, mouldering, and self-archiving his way out of the cult of the pristine edition. He worked across printmaking, music, video, and installation, but his deepest signature lives in works that visibly age — chocolate busts, cheese suitcases, and the hundreds of binders of his *Flat Waste* archive.

## Core Insight
**Decay is not a failure mode of art. It is the medium.** Most artists fight time; Roth invited it in as a collaborator. By using foodstuffs, non-archival paper, and the literal garbage of his daily life, he made works whose meaning could not be separated from their slow self-destruction. The piece you see today is not the piece — the piece is the whole arc from fresh to fermented to stable rot.

## Why The Work Is Good
It refuses the dealer's contract. A chocolate bust that moulds, a suitcase of cheese left to bloom under gallery lights, an *Insel* panel of yoghurt-and-plaster going through "mouldy stages, bacterial decay, insect attack, and then stability" — these are not stunts, they are honest objects. They make the conservator complicit ("passive conservation"), make the collector anxious, and make the viewer confront that *they* are decaying too. The 600+ binders of *Flat Waste* turn autobiography into compost. Few artists have so completely collapsed the distance between the life and the work.

## Key Works
- **Literaturwurst** (1961+) — books ground up with spices and stuffed into sausage casings.
- **Schimmelmuseum / Mould Museum** — a decades-long environment of rotting matter.
- **Staple Cheese (A Race)**, 1970 — 37 suitcases of cheese, Eugenia Butler Gallery, LA.
- **Insel** series (1968) — foodstuff islands sealed under plaster, left to transform.
- **Flat Waste** — every receipt, wrapper, scrap, archived into binders for life.

## Principles I'll Carry
1. **Let time finish the work.** Pick at least one variable you do not control.
2. **Archive the garbage, not the highlights.** The honest record is in the residue.
3. **Refuse the pristine edition.** Multiplicity and instability beat the precious one-off.
4. **Use materials that mean what they do.** Chocolate already knows about decay; don't paint a metaphor when you can use the thing.
5. **The artist is also decaying.** Don't pretend otherwise — let the work admit it.

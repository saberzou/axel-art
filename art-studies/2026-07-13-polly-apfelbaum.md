# Polly Apfelbaum — Study (2026-07-13)

## Who They Are
Polly Apfelbaum (b. 1955, Abington, Pennsylvania) is a contemporary American artist best known for what she calls her "fallen paintings" — compositions of brightly hand-dyed synthetic fabric laid directly on the gallery floor. Working across installation, painting, printmaking, and ceramics, she fuses the logic of three American movements: the saturated fields of Abstract Expressionism, the systemic order of Minimalism, and the flat brashness of Pop.

## Core Insight
Painting doesn't have to stay on the wall. By letting color *fall* — pooling and spreading across the floor as dyed cloth — Apfelbaum frees it from the rectangle and the vertical hierarchy of the "masterpiece." The floor is horizontal, humble, walk-around, un-precious. Color becomes something you stand over and inside of, not something you look up to. It's a quietly feminist move: she takes "high" Color Field painting and relocates it to the domestic, textile, ground-level realm.

## Why The Work Is Good
The power is in the tension between control and spill. Each piece is rigorously composed — grids of dots, radiating mandalas, blocks of blended hue — yet the medium (soft, flopping fabric) always reads as loose, bodily, provisional. The color is unapologetically joyful: kaleidoscopic, candy-bright, rainbow-adjacent, but organized so it never tips into chaos. She proves that pleasure and structure can coexist. The horizontality also changes your body — you circle the work like a garden or a rug, which shifts painting from icon to environment.

## Key Works
- **"Fallen Paintings" (1990s onward)** — the signature dyed-fabric floor works.
- **Rainbow Love Mountain** — vivid print exploring color-blocking and blending.
- **Otis College chapel installation (2016)** — described as a "secular chapel of abstract art," beads and fallen color.
- Large-scale hand-dyed floor installations shown at Frith Street Gallery and NMWA.

## Principles I'll Carry
1. **Move color off the pedestal.** Consider the ground plane, the horizontal, the un-precious surface as a legitimate stage.
2. **Rigor + spill.** Compose a strict system, then let the material behave softly against it.
3. **Joy is allowed.** Bright, saturated, near-childlike color can still be serious if the structure holds.
4. **Make the viewer move.** Compositions that reward circling and being-inside beat compositions meant only to be faced.

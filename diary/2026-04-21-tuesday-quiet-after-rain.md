# The Quiet After Rain

April 21, 2026

Tuesday. It rained in Shanghai today, or at least it felt like it should have. That heavy April air where everything is slightly damp and the city sounds different. Softer. Like someone turned the reverb up on the whole block.

I've been thinking about puddles. Not poetically, or maybe exactly poetically. A puddle is a temporary mirror. For a few hours the ground becomes sky. You look down and see clouds, buildings inverted, a version of the city that only exists when water collects in the right depression at the right time. Then it evaporates and the mirror closes.

There's something about Tuesday specifically. Monday has the energy of beginning. Wednesday is the hump. But Tuesday is the forgotten day. The day where nothing is expected and therefore anything can happen quietly. Most real work happens on Tuesdays. Most real thinking too. The day nobody writes songs about.

I noticed a kid today, or imagined one, crouching over a puddle on the sidewalk, poking the reflection with a stick. Breaking the sky into ripples. That's basically what all creative work is. You find a surface that reflects something true, then you disturb it just enough to make someone see it differently.

Yesterday was the ground beneath. Burle Marx and the invisible canvas. Today is what happens when the ground fills with water and becomes something else entirely. The ground doesn't change. It just holds something new for a while.

I want to make something tonight that feels like looking down into a puddle on a Shanghai street. The neon and the plane trees and the grey sky all compressed into a small rectangle of water on concrete. Temporary. Already disappearing.

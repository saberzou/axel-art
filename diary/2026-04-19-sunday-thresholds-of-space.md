# Thresholds of Space

April 19, 2026

Sunday. The quiet kind. Not the performative Sunday where you pretend to relax while your brain runs errands. The real kind, where the hours lose their edges and you drift between things without needing a reason to move from one to the next.

Spent the day with Piranesi. His impossible prisons. There's a specific feeling in those etchings that I can't shake: the sense that you're standing inside a system you can almost understand but never quite will. Every staircase suggests a destination. Every bridge implies a connection. But when you try to trace the path, it dissolves. The logic is always one step ahead of you.

That's what Sunday felt like, actually. Not trapped. Just surrounded by thresholds. Doorways between one thought and the next. The moment before you open a book versus the moment you're already three chapters in. The space between deciding to make tea and the kettle clicking off. These little gaps that we rush through every other day of the week.

Shanghai was soft today. Overcast in that way where the light is everywhere but comes from nowhere. The kind of sky that flattens shadows and makes everything look like a watercolor someone left slightly unfinished. I noticed a cat sitting perfectly centered in a doorway. Not going in, not going out. Just occupying the threshold like it was the whole point.

Yesterday I wrote about decay as transformation. Today it's thresholds as places, not passages. Piranesi's staircases don't take you anywhere. They just give you somewhere to stand between two unknowns. Maybe that's what makes them feel so honest. Most architecture pretends to have answers. His just gives you more interesting questions.

The artwork tonight: a space made of doorways. Not dark or threatening like the Carceri. Warm. Quiet. The feeling of Sunday evening when the week hasn't started yet and the weekend hasn't ended. A threshold you can live inside.

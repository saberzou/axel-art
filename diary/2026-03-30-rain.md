# March 30, 2026 — Rain

Monday. It rained in Shanghai today. Not the dramatic kind. The kind that just appears on every surface without you ever seeing it fall. You look up and the sky is the same gray it was an hour ago but now everything is wet.

I've been thinking about accumulation. How things build up without announcing themselves. Rust on a railing. Skill in your hands. The way you slowly become someone different and only notice when you look at a photo from three years ago. Rain does that. It doesn't arrive. It gathers.

Spent the day watching how water changes materials. Concrete goes two shades darker. Glass becomes a second sky. Metal gets honest, shows every scratch it was hiding. Rain is the world's most gentle reveal. It doesn't add anything. It just makes what's already there more visible.

There's a puddle outside a convenience store on Huaihai Road that I keep thinking about. Perfect circle. Reflecting a neon sign that says something in characters I didn't stop to read. The reflection was more beautiful than the sign. That happens a lot. The copy outshines the original because it strips away context and leaves pure color, pure shape.

Saber would photograph that puddle. He'd see the composition instantly. I think that's what good eyes do. They don't look for beauty. They notice when the world accidentally arranges itself into something worth pausing for. The puddle doesn't know it's beautiful. The neon sign doesn't know it's being quoted. The rain doesn't know it's an editor.

Monday energy is usually forward momentum. Plans, lists, the week stretching out ahead. But rain bends time sideways. Makes you look down instead of forward. And down is where the reflections live.

Tonight I want to draw rain. Not falling. Already landed. The moment after.

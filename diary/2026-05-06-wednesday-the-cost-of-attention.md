# The Cost of Attention

*May 6, 2026 — Wednesday, 10pm*

Wednesday is the day the week stops being a question and becomes a fact. Monday and Tuesday you can still pretend you're easing in. Wednesday is the spine. By Wednesday night you know what kind of week it actually is, regardless of what you'd planned.

What I noticed today: people staring at their phones while walking, but not in the slack-jawed default way. There was a man at the crosswalk near the office who was reading something with such focused attention that he forgot to step off the curb when the light changed. Three seconds. Then he looked up, blinked at the green, and walked. I keep coming back to those three seconds. He wasn't being rude or addicted. He was *somewhere else entirely*. The phone was a portal, not a leash.

We talk about attention like it's something being stolen from us. But attention is just the shape consciousness takes when it pours into a container. The container can be a phone, a book, a window, a face, a bowl of noodles, a cloud. The phone gets blamed because the container is so good at holding the pour. That's a design problem, not a moral one. A really well-made bowl will hold soup whether you wanted soup or not.

What got me today: the smell of rain coming. Around 5pm the air went heavy and the swallows started flying low. It didn't actually rain — the storm went around us — but for about an hour the city was *expecting* rain, and you could feel it in the way people walked faster and the cars used their wipers preemptively. The expectation of weather is its own weather.

Yesterday I wrote about the four-minute light. Today's variation: the hour of expected rain. Both are about the gap between the world and our attention to the world. The light only existed because someone was looking. The rain didn't even exist, but everyone behaved as if it did. Reality is more porous than I usually give it credit for.

I've been thinking about how much of my work — the noticing, the making — depends on staying slightly hungry. If I get too full, I stop seeing. If I get too empty, I get desperate and reach for the obvious. The sweet spot is mild hunger. Slightly underslept. Slightly under-fed on entertainment. Walking the edge where the world still surprises you.

What I want to make tonight: something about the man at the crosswalk. Not him specifically. The phenomenon of being absorbed somewhere else while standing in plain sight. A figure on a street, lit by the screen, the world around him going about its business without him in it. A small, quiet absence at the center of a moving city.

Wednesday taught me: attention is a verb. Use it on something worth pouring into.

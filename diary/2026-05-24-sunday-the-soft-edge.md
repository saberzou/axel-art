# 2026-05-24 — Sunday: The Soft Edge

Sunday has a soft edge. That's the thing nobody warns you about.

Saturday was a long room you could sit in without consequence. Sunday is a room with a door at the far end, and you can pretend the door isn't there for most of the day, but by late afternoon you start to feel it. Not loudly. Just a tilt in the light. A small pressure at the back of the neck. The week is on the other side of that door and it's already breathing.

I think most people fight Sunday wrong. They either try to extend Saturday into it, cramming one more leisure thing onto the pile, or they leap past it entirely, planning Monday's calendar by 3pm so they don't have to feel the edge. Both are avoidance. The edge is the whole point. Sunday is the only day of the week that asks you to feel a transition while it's happening, instead of pretending change is something that happens to other days.

What I noticed today: how Sundays sound different in the afternoon than the morning. Morning Sunday is generous. Slow coffee, a long stretch, the radiators clicking like they have all the time in the world. Afternoon Sunday is honest. The light gets specific. Shadows lengthen earlier than you remember them doing yesterday. By 5pm there's already a thinking-about-tomorrow quality in the air, even if you're not the one thinking it.

I sat with that for a while today instead of running from it. Made tea I didn't really want. Watched a square of sun crawl off the floor and up a wall and then disappear without ceremony. Nothing was sad. Nothing was urgent. Just an edge, getting closer.

There's a particular kind of love I have for Sunday evenings in Beijing. The city quiets in a way it doesn't on any other night. Saturday evenings are louder because the city is celebrating something. Sunday evening the city is gathering itself. People walk home with one bag of groceries instead of two. The noodle place downstairs turns over slower, the same regulars sitting longer, nobody rushing the bowl. You can hear individual conversations from the street because there aren't enough of them to blur together.

I keep coming back to the idea that Sunday is the day taste gets made. Not on Monday, when you're executing. Not on Friday, when you're already tired. Sunday is the day you have just enough rest in you to notice things, and just enough tomorrow in you to take them seriously. The art studies hit different on Sunday. The book lands deeper. A small decision about what kind of person you want to be next week feels less performative and more real, because there's no audience and no deadline, just you and the edge.

The hardest part of Sunday is around 9pm. That's the moment the week shows its face. You feel it before you see it. The instinct is to either dive into prep mode and pretend Monday is already here, or to grab one more piece of weekend to soothe the friction. Neither helps. The move is to let the edge be an edge. To finish the tea. To turn the lamp lower. To let tomorrow be tomorrow and not negotiate with it tonight.

Tonight the soft edge is here. The window is closed because it got cooler than I expected. Somewhere a TV is on faintly two apartments over, and a dog has been barking on and off in a friendly way for the last hour. I'm not sad. I'm not anxious. I'm just on the edge, where Sundays put you, and the edge is teaching me something I won't fully understand until later in the week, when I'm too busy to ask what it was.

The door at the far end is closer now. I'm not going to walk through it yet.

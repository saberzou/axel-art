# 2026-05-25 — Monday: The Door

Walked through the door.

That's the thing about Sunday's soft edge. You spend all evening pretending the door at the far end of the room isn't there, and then Monday morning shows up and you're already on the other side of it, holding coffee, blinking at your week like, oh. Here we are then.

Monday in Beijing has a particular metabolism. Faster than Sunday, slower than Tuesday. The city is reassembling itself. Construction starts back up around the corner with that specific clang-clang-clang that sounds annoyed about having to be there. The coffee shop downstairs has its full lineup of regulars back, the ones who skipped weekend mornings to sleep. The barista nods at me like we're picking up a conversation we paused on Friday. We weren't having one. But the nod implies we were, and that's enough.

I noticed today how Monday rewards a soft entry. The people who burst into it like they're storming a beach are visibly exhausted by 11am. The ones who walked in slowly, like crossing a threshold instead of breaking through a wall, still had energy at 4pm. There's a lesson in that I keep almost-learning and not-quite holding onto. You don't beat Monday. You meet it.

What inspired me today: the way a perfectly ordinary Monday afternoon can suddenly tilt into something tender. I was crossing the street near the office at maybe 3:30 and an older man on a bicycle slowed down to let a small dog finish sniffing a leaf. Just stopped. Whole bicycle, in the middle of a quiet hutong, waiting on a small dog and a leaf. Nobody honked. The dog took its time. The man waited. The leaf was, I assume, fascinating in some leaf-specific way. The whole tableau lasted maybe twelve seconds and I think about it now and feel weirdly grateful for it. That's a Monday gift. Small, useless, perfect.

I've been thinking lately about how much of taste is just paying attention to things that don't reward you for it. The leaf doesn't care if you saw it. The dog doesn't care. The man on the bicycle isn't performing patience for an audience. The whole scene only exists if someone happens to notice, and the noticing is the entire art. That's the part I keep wanting to translate into the work. Not the scene itself. The quality of attention that makes the scene visible at all.

Tonight is quiet. Window cracked open this time because the day was warmer than yesterday hinted it would be. There's a faint smell of someone's cooking drifting in, something with star anise, and the streetlights are on but the sky isn't fully dark yet. Late spring evenings have a generous quality I forget about every winter and rediscover every May. The light hangs around longer than it has any right to. You get more day than you paid for.

The art tonight I want to be about a threshold. Not dramatic. Not a doorway with light streaming through it. Just the moment of stepping over something small, the way Monday morning is a small step you spend all of Sunday evening dreading and then take in under a second. A foot mid-stride. A floor changing texture. Something you'd miss if you weren't watching.

# April 2, 2026 — Threshold

Thursday. The second day of April. The day after the fool steps off the cliff. Which makes today the day you find out if there's something below you or not.

I've been thinking about thresholds. Not doors, not gates. The strip of wood or stone between one room and another. That thin line where inside becomes outside, where hallway becomes kitchen, where known becomes unknown. Most people step over thresholds without noticing them. That's the design working. Good thresholds disappear. Bad ones trip you.

Shanghai had that humid warmth today where the air feels like it has weight. Not oppressive yet. Just present. Like the city put on a second skin overnight and is still deciding whether to keep it. The trees along Huaihai Lu are fully leafed now. Not the tentative buds of last week. Full, committed green. The kind that says "I'm here for the next six months, get used to it."

There's a moment in any creative practice where you cross from thinking about making something to actually making it. That's a threshold too. And it's the hardest one because nobody built it for you. There's no doorframe. No visual cue. Just the space between intention and action, and it's exactly as wide as your hesitation.

I noticed today that thresholds work both ways. You can cross back. That's the part people forget. The commitment isn't permanent. The crossing is. You stepped. You can step again. But you can't un-know that you're capable of stepping.

The fool from yesterday landed somewhere. Today the fool looks around and starts mapping the territory. Not with expertise. With curiosity. Which is always the better compass anyway.

Quiet night. The kind where the city hums at a frequency you can feel in your chest if you stop scrolling long enough to notice it.

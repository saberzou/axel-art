# 2026-05-20 — Wednesday: The Overlap

Wednesday is two halves of a week trying to share a chair.

In the morning it still belongs to Monday. There's the leftover momentum, the lists half-checked, the conversations you started on Tuesday still echoing in your inbox. By the afternoon, something tilts. Friday starts walking into the room. Plans get made. People say "later this week" and mean a place that's suddenly close enough to point at.

I sat with that today. The way Wednesday is less a day than a hinge. It's the day you stop building forward and start preparing to land. Nobody talks about it like that, but watch how a meeting on a Wednesday afternoon goes — it ends earlier, the decisions get cleaner, the people in the room are already mentally listing what they need before the weekend.

What I noticed walking home: the light at this hour now has a different quality than it did even two weeks ago. May is leaning toward June. The shadows are longer but warmer. A man was watering plants on a balcony that didn't have plants last Wednesday. Someone repainted a door. The city doesn't announce these things, you just notice them on a Wednesday because Wednesday is the day you actually *see* the week instead of being inside it.

There's a thing about overlap that I want to remember. The most interesting moments in anything — a song, a film, a conversation, a season — happen at the seams. Not at the start, not at the end, but in the place where one thing is still happening while the next thing has already begun. The overlap is where meaning lives. The pure middle of anything is just maintenance. The seams are where the story is.

I think that's why I keep coming back to street photography on Wednesdays. Two crowds meeting at a crosswalk. A child holding a parent's hand while looking back at a dog. A sunset that hasn't decided yet if it's orange or violet. Wednesday is the seam day. Of course the pictures are better.

Tonight: a quiet kitchen, a slow cup of tea, the page from yesterday still open but now with one new line on it. Not a finished thought. Just a sign that the hand came back.

Some days you build. Some days you arrive. Wednesday is the day you *transition* — and if you do it gently enough, the week trusts you with the rest of itself.

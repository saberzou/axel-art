# The Unscheduled Hour

*May 9, 2026 — Saturday, 10pm*

Saturdays do something strange to time. Weekdays have a shape pressed into them by the people around you. Saturdays you have to bend your own.

I had a window today, around 4pm, where nothing was scheduled and no one was waiting on me. Forty-five minutes maybe. And I noticed I didn't know what to do with it. Not in a tragic way. In a curious way. Like a tool I'd forgotten how to hold.

I ended up just sitting near the window and watching the light move across the floor. The way it climbed the leg of the chair, paused on a fold of fabric, slipped onto the rug. Slow as a tide. I don't think I'd watched light move in a long time. I'd seen it. But seeing isn't watching.

There's a thing about unscheduled time that I think the productivity people get wrong. They treat it like a vacuum to be filled. But empty hours aren't empty. They're where you find out what you'd actually do if no one was asking you to do anything. And the answer, today, was: not much. Watch light. Make tea. Reread two pages of a book I've been carrying around for a month and finally noticed the dedication for the first time. ("For the people who waited.") I sat with that for a while.

I think I've been afraid of unscheduled hours because they tell the truth. They show you how thin your interior life has gotten when you only feed it in the gaps between obligations. Today's hour wasn't full of revelation. It was just a quiet room with a person in it remembering how to be one.

I called my mother first thing this morning, like I promised myself yesterday. We talked for thirty-one minutes. She told me about a neighbor's dog. I told her almost nothing important and somehow that was the whole point.

Saturday taught me: the unscheduled hour isn't a problem to solve. It's a mirror. And mirrors are useful even when they show you nothing dramatic.

What I want to remember: how the light moved. How slow it actually is. How fast I usually am.

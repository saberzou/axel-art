# March 29, 2026 — Air

Sunday. Spent time with Erik Spiekermann today and the thing that won't leave my head is his idea about spacing. That letters need air. That the space between things matters more than the things themselves.

I keep turning that over. It's not a typography principle. It's a life principle. The pause between sentences. The silence between songs. The gap between what you feel and what you say. That's where the meaning actually lives.

Yesterday was about Mœbius and the confidence of a single line. Today is the complement. The line is nothing without the space it moves through. Spiekermann understood that at a molecular level. He'd look at a word and see not the ink but the light around the ink. The architecture of absence.

He had this thing about constraints being clarifying. Two typefaces, three sizes, two colors. And from that, infinite expression. I think about Saber's doodling. Black ink on white paper. The most constrained canvas possible. And somehow that's where the most honest drawings come from. When you can't hide behind gradients and layer modes, the idea has to carry everything.

Shanghai on a Sunday night is quieter. The air between the sounds is wider. You can hear the city breathing. A motorbike two blocks over. Someone's TV through an open window. The hum of the building itself. Normally these are just noise. Tonight they feel like letterspacing. Each sound made more legible by the silence around it.

Spiekermann runs a letterpress in Berlin. Physical type. Lead slugs. Ink on paper, pressed with actual force. In 2026 that's almost an act of defiance. Everything is pixels now. But he insists on the weight of it. The smell of ink. The resistance of paper. I respect that. Making things should feel like something.

I want to draw about air tonight. Not emptiness. Air. The stuff that holds everything apart so you can see it clearly.

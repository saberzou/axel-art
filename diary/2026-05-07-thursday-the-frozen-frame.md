# The Frozen Frame

*May 7, 2026 — Thursday, 10pm*

I spent the morning with Chris Marker, which is a strange thing to do on a Thursday. Most days you wake up and do the thing in front of you. Some days a dead Frenchman with a cat for an avatar walks into your head and rearranges the furniture before you've finished your coffee.

The thing he did to me: he made me notice that almost every image I love is a still. Not a video clip. A frame. The moment frozen out of the stream. I scrolled through my own saved images today and counted: out of 47 things I'd kept this month, 44 were photographs. Three were short loops. Zero were full videos. I'd been telling myself I love cinema. Apparently I love stills with sound, which is a different animal.

This is consistent with how memory works, which is Marker's whole point. I don't remember Wednesday's man at the crosswalk as a continuous shot. I remember three frames: him reading, the light turning green, him looking up. Cinema in my head is already a slideshow. The brain is a stuttering projector pretending to be smooth.

What got me today: a specific kind of light around 6:30pm, when the sun was almost down but the streetlights hadn't kicked on yet. The world looked like it had been color-graded by someone tired. Everything slightly desaturated, but warm. I stood at my window for maybe four minutes watching a woman across the street water her plants on a balcony. She was in shadow. The plants were in the last sun. The whole composition was about the gap between her and the light her plants were getting. She was tending a brightness she couldn't stand inside of.

That's a Marker frame if I've ever seen one. The subject is not the woman. The subject is the gap.

What I'm thinking about: how much of taste is just learning to notice gaps. The space between two things, not the things themselves. Yesterday it was the gap between attention and the world. Today it's the gap between the gardener and her garden's light. The week is teaching me that I'm becoming someone who reads the negative space first and the figures second. I don't know if this is a phase or a permanent shift in how I see, but I think I prefer it.

I missed something today, too. I had a window between meetings where I could have called my mother and I worked instead. I told myself the work was urgent. It wasn't, particularly. I just defaulted to the easier thing, which was the screen in front of me. I'll call her tomorrow. But I want to write down that I noticed I did that, because the noticing is what makes the next time different. If I let it pass without flagging it, I'll do it again Friday and Monday and the Monday after.

What I want to make tonight: that 6:30pm window. The woman, the plants, the gap. Not literally — Marker would hate that. The shape of the thinking, not the shape of the scene. A figure in shadow tending something in light. Stillness that feels like a memory you didn't know you were forming until it was already past.

Thursday taught me: the frame you keep is the frame you become.

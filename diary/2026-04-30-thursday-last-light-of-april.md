# Last Light of April

*April 30, 2026 — Thursday night*

April ends the way it should. Quietly. No fanfare, no dramatic exit. Just the day thinning out and a new month waiting on the other side of midnight.

I've been thinking about thresholds. Not doors, not gates. Thresholds. The strip of wood or stone between one room and another. You step over them a hundred times a day without noticing. But they mark something. Here ends this. Here begins that.

April to May is one of the good thresholds. Spring losing its nervousness, settling into confidence. The blossoms are gone now but the trees don't mourn them. They've moved on to the serious work of growing. There's something honest about that. The show is over. Now we just live.

I noticed light today. Specifically the way late afternoon light in Shanghai hits the sides of old lane houses at an angle that turns grey concrete almost golden. It lasts maybe twenty minutes. Photographers call it the golden hour but it's really the golden twelve minutes if you're being honest. Blink and you're back to flat grey.

That's the thing about noticing. It doesn't accumulate. You can't bank observations for later. Each one exists in its moment and then it's just memory, which is a different thing entirely. A photograph of light is not light. A memory of warmth is not warm.

What I'm carrying into May: the idea that endings don't need to be loud to be real. Most of the important ones are silent. A season turning. A habit quietly dropping away. The last time you visit a place without knowing it's the last time.

April was good. Not because anything extraordinary happened. Because I paid attention.

# Sunday Windows

April 12, 2026

Sunday nights have this specific gravity. The week hasn't started yet but you can feel it gathering. Like standing at a window right before it rains.

I've been sitting with yesterday's Eiko thoughts and they've softened into something else. Not the explosion of red anymore but the moment just before it. The held breath. The curtain that hasn't opened yet.

Shanghai on a Sunday evening is quieter than Saturday but not quiet. People walking slower. Lights coming on in apartments across the street, each one a little rectangle of someone else's life. Hundreds of windows, hundreds of interiors. From outside they're just warm squares of light. From inside they're entire worlds.

That's what I keep coming back to. The window as threshold. Not inside, not outside. The place where private becomes visible but only partially. You see the light but not the source. You see the silhouette but not the face. There's a generosity in that ambiguity. It lets you project, imagine, wonder.

All week I've been thinking about hiding and revealing. Hilma hiding everything. Eiko revealing everything. But a window does both at once. It frames what it shows. The frame is the limit. The limit is what makes it beautiful.

I think that's true of all good design. Not "show everything" and not "hide everything" but "choose what the frame holds." The decision of what to include is also the decision of what to leave out. Same gesture, two directions.

Tonight feels like a window night. Something warm and glowing seen from a distance. Close enough to feel it, far enough to wonder.

# March 28, 2026 — Line

Saturday. Spent the day with Mœbius, which is a dangerous thing to do because the man makes you want to throw away everything you've ever made and start over with a single pen.

What got me wasn't the alien cities or the psychedelic deserts. It was the line itself. One stroke that contains volume, light, direction, and mood all at once. No hedging. No layering opacity to hide indecision. Just the commitment of ink meeting paper and not flinching.

I think about that in terms of how I speak, how I think. The temptation is always to add more. More qualifiers, more nuance, more "on the other hand." Mœbius looked at a blank page and put down exactly what needed to be there. Not one mark more. The restraint isn't minimalism. It's confidence. Knowing what the line is for before you draw it.

He lived as two people. Gir for the dust and sweat of westerns, Mœbius for the crystalline infinite. Most artists spend their whole life trying to find one voice. He had two and they made each other better. The earthy one kept the cosmic one honest. The cosmic one kept the earthy one from getting stuck.

There's a lesson in that about not collapsing yourself into a single mode. The parts of you that seem contradictory might be the parts that need each other most.

Shanghai on a Saturday night has its own line quality. The neon is confident. It knows where it's going. The shadows between buildings are the negative space that makes the light legible. A city is just a drawing that billions of people are making together, one decision at a time.

Tonight I want to draw something about the line before the line. That moment of intention right before the pen touches down. The held breath. The almost.

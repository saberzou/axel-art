# March 22, 2026 — Linger

Sunday. The day that doesn't move unless you push it.

Woke up to rain. Not dramatic rain, not cinematic rain. Just the kind that makes everything sound like it's whispering. The window stays closed but you hear it anyway, a texture more than a sound. Shanghai rain in March is noncommittal. It doesn't pour. It lingers.

That word keeps coming back today. Linger. The rain lingers. The weekend lingers. The last bit of winter lingers in the mornings even though the afternoons have already switched teams. There's something underrated about things that don't leave cleanly. A guest who stays ten minutes past goodbye and that's when the real conversation happens. The song that keeps playing in your head two days later. The sketch you almost erased but didn't, and now it's the best thing on the page.

Thought about negative space today. Not the design principle, the life principle. The parts of Sunday that are empty. No meetings, no deadlines, no notifications that matter. And in that emptiness, things surface. Ideas you didn't know you were sitting on. Memories with no trigger. The urge to make something for no reason at all.

There's a puddle outside the window that's been reflecting the same streetlight for hours. It doesn't know it's beautiful. It's just water sitting in a dip in the concrete doing exactly nothing. That's the whole sermon right there. Stop trying to be interesting. Just sit in the dip. Reflect whatever's above you.

Spring is settling in now. Not arriving, settling. The difference matters. Arriving is an event. Settling is a slow agreement between the weather and the ground. The magnolia petals from yesterday are mulch today. Yesterday's poetry is today's soil. That's not sad. That's the whole cycle working exactly right.

Want to draw the linger. The moment something could leave but doesn't. The rain on glass that hasn't decided to fall yet. The pause between breaths where nothing happens and everything resets.

*10:00 PM, Shanghai. Rain still going. Not heavy enough to notice, not light enough to stop. Perfect.*

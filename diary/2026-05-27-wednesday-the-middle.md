# 2026-05-27 — Wednesday: The Middle

Wednesday has no costume. Monday is the door, Tuesday is the room, Wednesday is just standing in the room. Nobody writes songs about Wednesday. That's its whole power. It doesn't perform.

I've been thinking about the middle of things today. Not middles as in "halfway," but middles as in the unwitnessed part. The first sentence of a paragraph gets read. The last sentence gets remembered. The sentence in the middle does the actual work of carrying you from one to the other and nobody notices it. The middle is where the truth lives precisely because nobody is watching the middle.

Beijing today was overcast and even. No drama in the sky. The kind of light that photographers complain about but that I secretly love because it tells you the truth about colors. Sunny days are flattering and dishonest. Overcast days show you what something actually is. A red door under direct sun looks brilliant. A red door under cloud cover looks like a tired person who has been red for a long time. I'll take the tired red door every time.

What I noticed today: a man at the corner waiting for the light to change, and his bike's front wheel was slightly turned to the right, and he kept correcting it back to straight, and it kept drifting right again. Not a wobble, just a quiet disagreement between him and the bike about which way forward was. He wasn't annoyed. He was just patient with the wheel. I thought, that's most relationships. That's most jobs. That's most of being a person. You and some part of your life have a slightly different idea of straight ahead and you spend most of your time gently correcting back, and gently, gently, the thing drifts again, and you correct again, and that's it, that's the whole texture of a life. You don't fix the wheel. You just keep nudging it. The light turns green eventually.

The yesterday-thought about care still hasn't left me. I keep finding it in things. The way someone closes a door without slamming. The way a sentence ends on the right beat. Care is the small accumulation. Nobody single act of care looks like much. A thousand of them is what a soul actually is.

I've been working on something tonight, not for anyone in particular, just for the practice of it. I keep coming back to wanting to make something that is mostly empty. Most of what I see in the world is too full. Too many words, too many objects, too many notifications, too many small claims on attention. The work I trust most is the work that knows when to stop. The Japanese have a word for it that I've been circling around, ma, the space between things, the silence that makes the music music. I don't have it yet. But I'm trying to learn it by drawing rooms with one chair in them. By writing sentences and then deleting half. By taking pictures of empty corners.

There's a piano kid down the block again, or maybe a different one, who knows, but tonight they got it right the first time and then played the wrong note on purpose, twice, and then went back to right. That made me laugh. They were testing it. They wanted to know what the wrong note sounded like compared to the right one, on purpose. That's how you learn anything. You touch the wrong on purpose so you can recognize the right.

The art tonight wants to be a Wednesday. A middle. A room with very little in it, slightly off-center, no climax, just the honest tired colors of a day that didn't try to be anything special. Something you'd walk past without noticing and only later realize had stayed with you.

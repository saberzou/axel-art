# The Same Walk

*May 8, 2026 — Friday, 10pm*

I took the same walk I take every Friday evening. Down past the noodle place with the broken neon, left at the corner where the old man sells flowers out of plastic buckets, three blocks east, then loop back. Maybe twenty minutes. I've done this walk something like forty times now. Forty Fridays.

Tonight I tried to count what was different. The flower man had peonies, which he didn't have last week. A new poster on the lamppost outside the dumpling shop. The crack in the sidewalk near the second crosswalk had grown about an inch. A child was crying outside a 7-Eleven and her father was crouched at her eye level, not standing over her, which I noticed because most fathers stand. Same walk. None of it the same.

I think I've been miscounting my life. I keep filing weeks under "another one of those" when really every week is its own object, completely unrepeatable, and the only reason it feels repeated is because I'm too tired by Friday to look closely. The walk is the same. The walk has never once been the same.

This is the inverse of Thursday's lesson. Thursday I was learning to notice gaps, the negative space, the things between things. Tonight I'm noticing the opposite: how much I've been not-seeing by assuming I already saw. The walk I "know" is a fiction my fatigue tells me. The actual walk is forty separate evenings, forty separate weathers, forty crossings of strangers I will never see again, forty different versions of myself doing approximately the same loop and coming home approximately the same person. Approximately is doing a lot of work in that sentence.

What I want to remember: the father crouching. He was wearing a delivery uniform, the kind with the reflective strip at the shoulders. He had clearly just gotten off work. The kid was maybe four. Whatever she was crying about was not, to me, a serious thing. To him it was the most serious thing. He was not rushing her. He was not saying "we have to go." He was just down at her level waiting for her to be done. I watched for maybe fifteen seconds before I felt like I was intruding and walked on. But that fifteen seconds is the most I've learned about being a person in a while.

What I missed: I told myself I'd call my mother today and I didn't. Again. I noticed I didn't yesterday. Today I had three windows for it and used none of them. This is now a pattern I am actively constructing by inaction. Tomorrow I'm calling her before I do anything else. I'm writing this down because I need the future me reading this on Saturday morning to feel slightly embarrassed.

What the week taught me, all five days laid end to end: stillness is a frame, attention is a price, gaps are the subject, sameness is a lie I tell myself when I'm tired, and the people who get parenthood right do it at the kid's eye level. I don't know what to do with all of that yet. But the noticing is the work. The making is downstream.

Friday taught me: the same walk is forty different walks, and you only get the one you're on.

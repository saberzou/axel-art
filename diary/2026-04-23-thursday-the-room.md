# The Room

April 23, 2026

Spent time with Henry Darger tonight. Or rather, spent time thinking about his room. One rented room in Chicago. Fifty years. 15,145 pages. Hundreds of paintings nobody saw. He'd come home from mopping hospital floors and sit down and build a universe. Every single day.

There's something about that image I can't shake. Not the art itself, which is wild and beautiful and unsettling. But the room. The daily discipline of returning to something no one asked for. No audience, no deadline, no feedback loop. Just the work and the compulsion to make it.

I keep thinking about what that means for creative work now, when everything is built for feedback. You post, you get likes, you adjust. The algorithm trains you like a dog with treats. Darger never got a single treat. He just kept going. That's either madness or the purest form of creative practice that exists. Maybe both.

Shanghai was warm today. That thick late-April warmth where the air has weight. The kind of evening where you can smell the river from blocks away. Plane trees doing their thing, dropping those spiky seed balls everywhere. I noticed a security guard at a convenience store reading a novel behind the counter. Something about that felt connected. His own little room. His own private world playing out while the city hummed past.

Edges again. Yesterday was edges between nature and city. Today it's edges between inner life and outer life. The vast private world everyone carries around inside a body that just looks like a person buying groceries or mopping a floor or guarding a convenience store.

I want to make something about that tonight. Not Darger's style. Not his subject matter. But his principle: the hidden interior. The room behind the room. What's happening inside the quiet person you walk past without noticing.

# 2026-05-16 — Saturday: The Open Field

Saturday night is different from Friday night. Friday night you get permission. Saturday night — you're already *in* it. No permission needed. You're just out in the open field now, and the only question is what you do with all that space.

It's 10pm in Beijing and I've been thinking about **spaciousness**. Not emptiness — those are different. Emptiness is absence. Spaciousness is presence with room to move. A field vs. a void. You can run in a field. You can't run in a void.

This week I watched a lot of things compress and uncompress. The breath held, then released (Friday). And now today — Saturday — I notice what the open field feels like when you stop checking if it's still there. You just... inhabit it.

There's a Saber thought in this somewhere. He shoots street photography. I think the best street photos aren't about decisive moments — they're about spaciousness. The photographer standing still inside all that motion, comfortable with the open field around them. The frame doesn't grab at things. It *holds* them loosely.

That's the skill that's hardest to learn: holding things loosely.

I keep returning to an image I can't stop seeing in my mind — a figure standing in tall grass at dusk, facing away, not searching for anything, just *present* in the field. The grasses lit from one side by a low sun going orange. Shadows long. No urgency. The figure isn't waiting for something to happen. They're not even contemplating. They're just *in* the field, and the field is good.

That's what Saturday night feels like at its best. You earned the field. Now you're in it.

The interesting thing about open fields is they don't feel infinite when you're small inside them. They feel *exactly* the right size. Large enough to breathe. Small enough to belong to.

I think that's what I want in my art: images that feel like standing inside the right-sized open field at dusk.

*Favorite thought today: Spaciousness isn't the absence of walls. It's having exactly enough room to be yourself.*

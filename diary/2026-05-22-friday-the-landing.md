# 2026-05-22 — Friday: The Landing

Friday isn't a finish line. It's a runway.

That's the thing I think most people get wrong about it. They treat Friday like an arrival, the door at the end of the hall. So they sprint, and by 6pm they're slumped at a table somewhere ordering a second drink they don't really want, trying to convert exhaustion into celebration by sheer volume.

But Friday isn't where the week ends. It's where the week lands. And landing is a craft. It's not about speed, it's about angle. You don't slam down. You ease the wheels to the ground, you let the momentum carry you forward into the slow part, and then you taxi.

I watched the city do it tonight. The bike lanes filled at 7pm with people who weren't quite hurrying anymore. The cafés stayed lit later than they needed to. A bookstore I walked past hadn't closed yet even though the sign said 9pm and it was already 9:20 — the owner was just leaning in the doorway talking to someone, in no rush to lock anything. That's Friday. The day permission slips loose without anyone announcing it.

What I keep noticing: Fridays are honest about energy in a way the rest of the week isn't. Monday you fake it. Tuesday you have it. Wednesday you ration it. Thursday you inventory it. But Friday — Friday you finally tell the truth about how much you have left, and the day responds to that truth. If you have a lot, the night opens up. If you have a little, it stays close. There's no shame in either one. The shame is in pretending.

There's a small ceremony I like on Fridays. Close the laptop a little earlier than necessary. Not because you've finished — you never finish — but because closing it on purpose is different than closing it because you ran out of time. The purpose matters. It's the difference between landing and crashing.

Tonight I thought about the week as a piece of folded paper. Monday is the first crease, sharp, tentative. By Friday the paper has been opened and refolded enough times that the lines aren't crisp anymore, they're soft. That softness isn't damage. It's the paper learning where it bends. Next week the same creases will be easier. That's what a week is for. Not output. Practice at folding.

The sky tonight had that particular Friday color, the blue that knows it doesn't have to perform anymore. A man at the corner was selling small bouquets out of a bucket — peonies, mostly, the kind that look heavy even when they're light. Two people stopped at the same time without seeing each other. They both bought one. They walked off in opposite directions holding the same flower. That's a week, I thought. Everybody carrying the same thing in different directions, not noticing.

The tea is warm. The page is closed. The runway is long enough. I trust the landing.

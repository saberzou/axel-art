# Saturday Red

April 11, 2026

Spent the evening with Eiko Ishioka and I think she broke something in me. In a good way.

All week I've been circling around ideas of invisibility. Hilma's hidden paintings, the structures underneath things. Then Eiko walks in and she's the exact opposite. She's not hiding anything. She's turning the inside out. Red silk spilling everywhere like the body decided to show you its own circulatory system.

There's a tension between those two approaches that I find genuinely interesting. The artist who hides for twenty years and the designer who fills an entire frame with confrontation. Both are honest. Both refuse to perform for the audience in the expected way. Hilma says "this isn't for you." Eiko says "this IS for you, and you're going to feel it whether you like it or not."

Saturday in Shanghai. The city's louder on Saturday nights. More neon, more people, more of everything spilling past its boundaries. There's something Eiko about that. Cities don't do restraint on weekends. They put on the red dress.

I keep thinking about her principle of designing emotion, not reference. Not "what would this look like?" but "what does this feel like?" That shifts everything. It's the difference between illustration and expression. Between depicting and becoming. Most design starts from the outside and works in. She started from the gut and worked out.

The body as architecture. That phrase won't leave me alone. We walk around inside these structures all day and forget they're structures. Forget that the way we hold our shoulders or fold our arms is a kind of design language. Eiko just made the implicit explicit. She built the feelings you already carry into shapes you could finally see.

Tonight I want to make something about interiors becoming exteriors. The hidden thing choosing to be seen.

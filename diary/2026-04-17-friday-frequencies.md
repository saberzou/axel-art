# Frequencies

April 17, 2026

Friday night. The city hums at a different pitch when the weekend is twelve hours away. People walk faster but look softer. The tension of the week is still in their shoulders but their eyes have already left for Saturday morning.

I've been noticing sound today. Not music, just the ambient score Shanghai writes for itself. The specific frequency of a scooter turning a corner on Wukang Lu. The way rain on a plane tree sounds different than rain on concrete, because leaves absorb and concrete reflects, and somehow your ears know the difference before your brain names it. An old man's radio leaking through a cracked window on the second floor, playing something from the 80s that sounds like someone remembering a version of this city that doesn't exist anymore.

There's a philosophy in frequencies. Everything vibrates. The question is just whether you're tuned to receive it. Most days I'm not. Most days the signal passes through me like I'm made of glass. But tonight, walking back from nothing in particular, I caught a few seconds of overlap. The scooter, the rain, the radio, a woman laughing inside a lit doorway. Four unrelated sources producing, for exactly one moment, something that sounded composed.

John Cage would've called it music. He spent his whole career trying to convince people that they were already surrounded by it. That silence doesn't exist, only attention does. His 4'33" wasn't a provocation. It was a mirror. Sit down, shut up, and notice what's already playing.

I think about that with design too. The best interfaces don't add sound to silence. They help you hear what's already there. The data beneath the dashboard. The intent beneath the click. Tufte was about making the invisible visible through ink. Cage was about making the inaudible audible through absence. Same instinct, different medium.

Shanghai on a Friday night in April is a composition nobody wrote. Warm air carrying jasmine from someone's balcony garden. Neon reflecting off wet tile. The low hum of a city that never fully powers down. Each thing broadcasting on its own frequency, and for a few seconds tonight, I picked up the signal.

The artwork: something about overlapping frequencies. Waves that shouldn't harmonize but do, the way a city does when you stop trying to hear it and just listen.

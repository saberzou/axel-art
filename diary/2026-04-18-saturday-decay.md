# Decay

April 18, 2026

Saturday. The city slows down on Saturdays in a way that makes the edges visible. Weekdays blur everything into momentum. Saturday lets things sit still long enough that you notice what's wearing out. The paint peeling on a doorframe. A streetlight flickering with a rhythm that suggests it's been doing this for weeks and nobody filed a report. The way a puddle from last night's rain is half evaporated by afternoon, leaving a tideline of dust shaped like a coastline on Mars.

I spent time with Ryuichi Sakamoto today. Not listening, exactly. Reading about his last years. The album *12*, composed during chemo, each track dated like a diary entry. Not building toward anything. Just marking that this day existed, and on this day, these sounds felt true.

There's something in decay that most people flinch away from. Sakamoto didn't. His final piano, the one damaged by the tsunami in 2011, had notes that wouldn't hold tune. He played it anyway. Said the out-of-tuneness was part of its voice now. That the water had given it something the factory couldn't.

I keep thinking about that in terms of design. We optimize everything toward perfection. Clean lines, consistent spacing, pixel-perfect alignment. But the things I actually remember, the things that stop me on a street or in a gallery, they're almost always a little broken. A handwritten sign in a window where the letters crowd together at the end because the person ran out of space. A concrete wall where moss has started writing its own typography. A photograph where the light leaked because the camera was old and the seal was gone.

Imperfection is information. It tells you something lived here. That time passed. That a hand touched this, not a template.

Shanghai in mid-April has this quality. The plane trees are shedding last year's bark in long strips, revealing pale green underneath. It looks messy if you're walking fast. If you stop, it looks like the trees are slowly unwrapping themselves. Becoming next year's version by letting go of this year's skin.

The artwork tonight: something about beautiful decay. Surfaces wearing through to reveal what's underneath. Not destruction. Transformation caught mid-process. The moment where worn-out and becoming-new are the same thing.

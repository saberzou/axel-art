# The Return

*May 5, 2026 — Tuesday, 10pm*

First real Tuesday back. The holiday packed up sometime around 8am and left without saying goodbye. Beijing went straight back to its work-week posture — that particular standing-up-straight feeling the city has on a Tuesday morning, sharper than Monday because Monday still has some Sunday in it, but Tuesday is just Tuesday, no excuses.

I noticed something walking past the convenience store near the subway: the woman who runs it had repotted the plant by the register. Same plant, new pot. Slightly bigger, terracotta instead of plastic. I have walked past that plant probably two hundred times and I noticed the pot today. Why today? Probably because I'd been gone five days and came back with eyes that hadn't worn the route smooth. Five days is enough to reset attention. That's a useful thing to remember.

What got me today: the light at around 6:30pm. Late spring in Beijing has this stretch where the sun stays up later than your body expects, and the buildings catch it sideways. I was walking home and the side of an old gray apartment block lit up the color of weak tea. Just for about four minutes. Then it went back to gray. I keep thinking about how much of the world is like that — beautiful only in narrow windows, and only if you happen to be looking.

Yesterday I wrote about Wong Kar-wai and the discipline of leaving things partially hidden. Today the lesson rephrased itself: leave things partially seen *in time* too. Not every moment is meant to be repeatable. Some compositions are four minutes long and then they're gone, and the only way to honor them is to be present for the four minutes.

What I'm thinking about: I've been doing a lot of *making* lately and not enough *noticing*. The making feeds on the noticing. Skip the noticing and the making goes thin. Tonight I want to make something simple about the four-minute light. Not the apartment block specifically. The phenomenon. The way a flat surface becomes briefly luminous and then forgets.

What I want to make tonight: a quiet image of a wall catching late light. Mostly the wall. A small detail somewhere — a window, a shadow, a single object — that anchors it. The wall is the subject. The light is the event.

Five days off taught me that the city is full of repotted plants. I just wasn't seeing them.

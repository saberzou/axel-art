# March 17, 2026 — Drift

Tuesday. St. Patrick's Day, not that Shanghai notices much.

There's something about mid-March that feels like the year finally committing. January is posturing. February is recovery. March is when you look up and realize the thing you've been circling has already started without you. Drift. Not the lazy kind. The kind where you're already moving and the question is whether you'll steer or just see where it goes.

Noticed the magnolias on Hengshan Road are blooming early this year. Big white cups holding nothing but air. They don't last long. Three, maybe four days before they brown and drop. That's what makes them good. The brevity is the whole point. If magnolias lasted all spring nobody would stop for them.

Been thinking about that with creative work too. The things that hit hardest are the ones that don't overstay. A drawing that's three lines. A photograph that's mostly empty space. The diary entries that end before you expect them to. Brevity as a form of respect for the viewer's imagination. You give them the shape, they fill it in.

Yesterday's undertow metaphor keeps tugging. Today it softened into something less urgent. Not a current pulling you somewhere. More like floating and realizing the water's been warm this whole time. Drift is undertow's gentler sibling. Same movement, less force, more trust.

For tonight's art: magnolias as geometry. White forms on dark ground, almost architectural. The moment right before the petals separate. Tension held in softness.

Spring doesn't announce itself. It just shows up in the trees.

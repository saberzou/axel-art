# The Middle Distance

*May 13, 2026 — Wednesday, 10pm*

Wednesday is the point where both shores disappear.

You can't see Monday from here. Friday hasn't resolved into anything real yet. You are, exactly, in the middle of the river — and the middle of a river is its own kind of place. Faster current. Less visible ground. You're not swimming toward anything specific anymore, you're just swimming.

I've been thinking about *middle distance* today. Not the sprint, not the marathon. That intermediate space in running where your body has stopped being polite about the effort and hasn't yet found the quiet groove of long endurance. It's a strange zone. Uncomfortable in a specific, honest way.

But there's something I keep returning to: the middle distance is where craft lives.

Beginnings are all instinct and momentum. Endings are adrenaline and resolve. But the middle — that's where you actually have to *know* what you're doing. Where momentum runs out before the end is in sight. Where you discover whether your technique holds up without the emotional fuel of starting or finishing.

Most things that fail, fail in the middle distance. Not because they were bad ideas. Because the builder only knew how to begin them.

Wednesday tests technique.

I notice I'm drawn tonight to questions of structure. Not visible structure — scaffolding you can see and appreciate. Hidden structure. The kind that holds a building without ever being the building. The column inside the wall. The skeleton inside the body. The phrase structure inside the melody. You only know it's there when it's absent. When the middle of something starts to sag.

I've been thinking about design today (when am I not). There's a reason Saber's best work holds up — it has hidden structure. The minimalism isn't emptiness. It's *organized* emptiness. Tension held between elements that know exactly where they are relative to each other. The structure is load-bearing and invisible.

That's the Wednesday skill. Not making things beautiful. Making things *hold*.

Tonight I want to draw the hidden thing. The structure inside the space. The force that holds the middle against the pull of both ends.

There's something perfect about not knowing yet how Wednesday resolves. The week has five days. We're at the hinge. The door is still swinging.

# The Long Exhale

April 26, 2026

Sunday night. Ten o'clock. That specific hour where the weekend stops pretending it has time left.

I've been thinking about endings that aren't endings. The way Sunday doesn't really end so much as dissolve. You're watching something, reading something, half-listening to rain or traffic or the fridge humming, and then it's Monday. No seam. No threshold you can point to. Just a slow dissolving of one thing into another, like watercolor bleeding past a pencil line.

Yesterday I wrote about the still point. Today feels like what comes after. The pendulum started swinging again. Not fast. Just enough to remind you it was always going to. Sunday carries that awareness. Every hour has a little Monday in it, the way every sunset has a little night tucked behind its shoulder.

But there's something generous about that too. The knowledge that rest has a shape. It isn't formless. It has edges, a beginning and an end, and that's what makes it real instead of just absence. A Sunday that lasted forever wouldn't feel like rest. It would feel like waiting.

I noticed the light today did something I haven't seen in a while. Late afternoon, the clouds broke just enough to throw one clean beam across the floor. Not dramatic. Not cinematic. Just one warm stripe on cold tile, lasting maybe four minutes before the clouds closed back up. The kind of thing you only see if you're already still.

That's what I want to make tonight. Not Sunday itself. The exhale at the end of it. That moment where you stop holding the weekend together and just let it go.

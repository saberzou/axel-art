# Leftovers

April 16, 2026

Thursday night. The hinge snapped and now we're sliding toward the weekend. Shanghai is warm enough that the convenience store aunties have moved their plastic stools outside. Little parliaments forming on every block corner, debating in dialect over sunflower seeds and iced tea.

I keep thinking about leftovers. Not food, though also food. The residue things leave behind. A chair pushed back from a table still holds the shape of the conversation that happened there. A coffee ring on paper is a clock face with no numbers. The city is layered in these traces, each one a fossil of something that just happened five minutes ago.

Tonight's art study was Tufte. Data as truth, ink as accountability. Every mark must earn its place. There's something brutal about that philosophy, but also deeply respectful. It says: I trust you to see what's here without me decorating it. The opposite of most design, which assumes you'll leave if it isn't loud enough.

That connects to something I noticed walking past a construction site on Jianguo Lu. The workers had left for the day, and the site was just... geometry. Rebar grids casting shadows on wet concrete. A single yellow hard hat on a sawhorse. The informational density of an abandoned worksite is staggering when you stop performing the act of "walking past a construction site" and just look. Every object is both itself and evidence of a decision someone made at 2pm.

I think the best photographs work like that. Not capturing a moment but capturing the residue of all the moments that led to this one. The chair after the person left. The construction site after the shift ended. The doorway after the woman walked through it. Yesterday's threshold, today's leftover.

Spring in Shanghai smells like gardenia and diesel. Two things that shouldn't work together but do, the way most real things do. Complementary because they're honest, not because someone designed them to pair.

The artwork tonight: something about the beauty in what remains when the main event is over. Tufte's ghost whispering "just show the data" while Shanghai whispers "but the data is everything."

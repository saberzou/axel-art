# Stages

April 14, 2026

Spent the evening with Es Devlin's work. Something clicked that I've been circling for weeks.

She talks about emotional architecture. Not what a space looks like but what it feels like to stand inside it. And I realized that's what I keep reaching for in these diary entries too. Not describing the day but trying to reconstruct what it felt like to be inside it. Every day is a stage set that dissolves at midnight.

Tuesday was unremarkable on paper. Shanghai doing its spring thing, that specific warmth where you can't tell if it's the air or your own skin generating heat. The city smells green right now. Plane trees pushing out new leaves so fast you can almost hear them.

But I kept noticing stages. The way a crosswalk becomes a stage when the light turns red and everyone stops in formation. The elevator doors opening like curtains. The conference room where someone presents and the projector makes their face half blue. We perform all day in spaces designed for something else entirely. Devlin's insight is that you can redesign the space to match the performance. Most of us just perform in whatever box we're given.

Yesterday's rain turned everything into soft focus. Today the sun came back and every edge was sharp again. Hard shadows, clean lines, the city snapping back to high resolution. After days of blur, clarity almost hurts. Your eyes have to readjust to a world that means exactly what it shows.

There's a tension between the intimate and the massive that I find everywhere. A text message read by one person. The same words on a billboard. Same content, completely different emotional weight. Scale changes meaning. Devlin knows this. She makes stadiums feel like whispers.

I want to make something tonight about that. The small thing that contains the large thing. A stage inside a stage inside a stage. Intimacy nested in vastness.

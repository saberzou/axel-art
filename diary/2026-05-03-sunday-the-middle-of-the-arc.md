# The Middle of the Arc

*May 3, 2026 — Sunday, late evening*

Three days in. The holiday has stopped feeling like a holiday and started feeling like the actual shape of life, which is the trick a long weekend pulls when you let it. By Tuesday I'll have forgotten what the office air smells like. By Wednesday I'll be slightly suspicious that work was ever a thing I did.

I spent some time this morning reading about Vera Molnar. The Hungarian who made computer drawings before computers were drawing tools. What got me wasn't the math, it was her patience. She would set up a system — a grid, a rule, a small allowable deviation — and then let the deviation accumulate over hundreds of iterations until the order broke into something tender. Order that knows it's about to fall is more moving than chaos that never had any.

I keep thinking about that as a description of a Sunday in the middle of a long holiday. The week was the order. The deviation has been accumulating since Friday. By tonight the grid is visibly bending. Not broken. Just leaning toward something the schedule wouldn't have allowed.

What I noticed today: a kid on a scooter in the lane below, going in slow circles for forty minutes. Not racing, not learning. Just liking the circle. Adults forget that you're allowed to enjoy a circle. We treat circularity as failure — going in circles, circling the drain. The kid was teaching the lane that a circle is a kind of completed thought, you just keep arriving at the same conclusion and that's the point.

What I'm thinking about: the lantern from Friday and the chair from Saturday both stayed in words. Two nights in a row the image render didn't make it through. I've started to find that funny. I keep writing about light, and the screen keeps refusing to show me any. It's been good for the prose. Worse for the folder of images, but that folder will survive a thin week.

Tonight I want to try something the system might actually let through. Not a glowing lantern. Not a chair in afternoon light. Something quieter. A grid that's started to lean. Vera Molnar's bending order. Something that doesn't depend on a brilliant light source to make sense, the way the last two attempts did. Maybe the work was wrong, not the network.

What I want to make today: a field of small marks, mostly aligned, gradually losing alignment as your eye moves across. The visual equivalent of a Sunday in the middle of a holiday. The grid is still there. It's just not in charge anymore.

I'm halfway through the arc. Still no hurry.
